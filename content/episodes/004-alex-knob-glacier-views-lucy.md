Alex Knob has been on my bucket list for ages. And I'm pretty sure Lucy was the first person to tell me about it, when she asked if I knew where it was. I'd never heard of it. Then I saw her go, and now I need to go too.

Lucy is a travel and adventure photographer based in Auckland. When we recorded she was in her last year of school, so her Instagram was taking a break (a temporary one, she hopes). I'd loved following her Wander Wednesdays, when she'd head somewhere new around Auckland every week.

So we start there, then get to the big one: Alex Knob above Fox Glacier, hiked early to beat the midday cloud. Plus the West Coast stops around it, and the South Island trips on her list.

## Wander Wednesdays around Auckland {#wander-wednesdays}

Lucy does a lot of day trips around Auckland.

I'm super passionate about that too, because we have so much here: Te Henga Walkway is one of my favourites, and there are all the islands in the [Hauraki Gulf](https://abigailhannah.nz/114-sea-kayaking-for-beginners-and-the-best-of-the-hauraki-gulf-with-bennett-lim/), and Oakley Creek waterfall.

Wander Wednesdays were Lucy's version. Pretty much every Wednesday after school, she'd take a super local trip, since she didn't have much time. She'd bus or bike somewhere, then, in her words:

> "go for a walk and absorb nature and share some of my adventures with people so they could see little parts of Auckland"

She tried to go somewhere new every week, and some weeks she'd bus into the city, catch the first bus that came and see where it took her. Her bigger trips are always planned, but her own style is a lot more spontaneous.

I used to look forward to it every week: where's Lucy going to be today?

Ask Lucy beaches or mountains and she'll say mountains, with a catch:

> "I kind of take the beach for granted and it's like a steady thing that I Never really think about so I would have to say mountains but I know my soul needs the beach."

I'm a beaches-in-summer, mountains-in-winter person. A cheat answer, but I'm going with it.

## Camping and tramping growing up {#growing-up-outdoors}

Lucy grew up camping every second weekend with her family, for years, so camping and tramping have always been part of her life. She was never at home, always out somewhere, often in the rain.

As they got older and their hiking got better, the trips grew. Short walks along the beach built up to the Pinnacles, then to the [Great Walks](https://abigailhannah.nz/10-great-walks-in-10-days/), and they could go further from home for longer. Most of it happened in the school holidays and on long weekends.

## Planning Alex Knob from Fox Glacier {#planning-alex-knob}

Lucy first saw Alex Knob on someone's Instagram, ages before she went. Then her family had two days at Fox Glacier, and they umm-ed and ah-ed about it for ages. The track was rated quite advanced, and not everyone in the group was an experienced hiker, so it was something safer or a giant long hike to see what happened.

It's also very weather dependent. You don't want to hike it in the torrential rain the [West Coast](https://abigailhannah.nz/064-west-coast-road-trip/) can throw at you.

The night before, they decided to go for it, got up super early, checked the forecast, and it looked okay.

Lucy's tip: the cloud lifts off the mountains in the morning, then comes back down on top of them around midday. For a clear view of the glacier, get up there early. She's not an early bird at all, so it was painful, and worth it.

That's a really good tip. We looked at doing it one summer from Wānaka, and there wasn't enough time to get there and hike up before the cloud rolled in at midday.

## Hiking Alex Knob for the glacier view {#hiking-alex-knob}

It was cold, by an Aucklander's standards. The group spread out on the way up, so Lucy went at her own pace, running and walking with music on. It was her first real alone time on the trip, and she needed it.

### The track

Alex Knob is steep. There are a few parts where you walk between tree roots, and it's steep in places, but most of the track is pretty decent. Lucy tracked it on Strava:

* **Distance:** 15.8 kilometres there and back.
* **Time:** 4 hours 11 minutes, running most of the way down.
* **Elevation:** over a thousand metres.

Lucy was a lot quicker than the DOC sign. I'm never on the shorter end of a DOC time: if DOC says four to five hours, it takes me five.

### Racing the cloud

Near the top, Lucy could see cloud coming in through the gaps in the trees. As soon as she got through the treeline, she could see it rolling in, so she sprinted the last stretch:

> "I didn't know whether it was covered or not so I was just running and hoping for the best."

At the top there was a bit of cloud on the mountains, but she could still see the glacier and got a few photos before it closed in. The others arrived in time to see about half.

There's no shelter above the trees, so it got cold fast once they stopped, though it was still sunny. Just as they were about to leave, a kea flew around right by them. Lucy wasn't quick enough for a photo.

### Camping at the top

Lucy wants to go back and camp at the top. She's seen photos of people camping up there, and she'd love it at golden hour, for sunrise or sunset.

I've seen [astrophotography](https://abigailhannah.nz/027-astrophotography-larryn-rae/) from up there too, with the stars and the glacier, and it looks like such a cool spot.

For hikers at Lucy's level, who aren't backcountry-ready, she reckons it's probably the best option for viewing the glacier. A heli hike onto the ice would be the other way, and Lucy would love it: she's never seen proper snow.

When I hike to glaciers, they always seem to be receding so fast, and I wonder if they'll still be there in 10 or 20 years. Maybe I'd better do Alex Knob soon.

## West Coast stops and a bucket list {#west-coast-and-bucket-list}

The West Coast is Lucy's favourite South Island region so far. They had no stormy days, and the only rain was going through Haast Pass. Around the hike they visited:

* **Hokitika Gorge.**
* **Lake Matheson**, though it was really windy, so no reflection, sadly.
* **Pancake Rocks**, and some caves just down the road that looked quite cool.
* **Greymouth.**
* **A seal colony**, looking down from a cliff at seals in the rock pools. They were so far away that the photos were taken through binoculars.

On our first South Island trip, we drove down the West Coast and didn't see the sun for days. I loved Pancake Rocks and Hokitika, but the glacier end was cloudy and wet, so I want to go back.

Her bucket list starts with two Great Walks, the [Routeburn](https://abigailhannah.nz/009-solo-travel-routeburn-track-emily/) and the full [Kepler](https://abigailhannah.nz/003-hiking-the-kepler-track-in-fiordland-with-dua/). Then Lake Marian, which she saw on my Instagram and didn't have time for on her road trip. When she finishes school, she wants to road trip the South Island with friends, revisit her favourite places and do some hikes, including [Mueller Hut](https://abigailhannah.nz/028-mueller-hut-backyard-travel-family/).

Lake Marian is so much prettier than I expected. It feels like you're in Canada, on a smaller scale. Highly recommend.
