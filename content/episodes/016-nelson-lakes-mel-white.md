Mel is one of the people I've met through the outdoor community on Instagram, and she knows Nelson Lakes like the back of her hand. It's her little slice of heaven, an hour from home, and the place that made her fall in love with hiking.

She's a self-taught photographer who grew up with a camera by her side, travelling around New Zealand as soon as she had a car. So we talk about her favourite hikes in Nelson Lakes, especially Angelus Hut (the only one I've done there, and it took me soooo long), her tips for hiking solo, trekking to Everest Base Camp at 18, and how the night sky got her into photography. Plus I tell the story of my very first attempt at astrophotography. It does not go well.

## Growing up with Aoraki and finding Nelson Lakes {#mel-story}

Every year after Christmas, for as long as Mel can remember, she spent a week camping at Lake Benmore: fishing, stargazing in the evenings and hanging out with family friends. On a good day the camp has a view of [Aoraki Mount Cook](https://abigailhannah.nz/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/), and she remembers running out to the fence every morning to check whether you could see it. That's why she loves that part of the country so much.

We were at Lake Benmore around Christmas this year, and the shores were packed with people camping and boats on the water. I was so jealous she grew up with that.

Mel cherished those trips, but she always looked at Aoraki without visiting. So she was the first to take herself there, and she keeps going back for the hiking.

Things changed when she left high school and bought a car. With a flexible job, she could take a couple of days and go somewhere, armed with a very basic point-and-shoot camera she'd been given for Christmas. Once she saw the hikes further south, she was driving seven hours on weekends to get to them. Most trips started solo, and the community she's built on Instagram turned them into group trips with friends she's met along the way.

When we recorded, she'd spent the last few months doing freelance photography: portraits, commercial shoots and wineries.

Growing up in Marlborough, she did mostly local trips, and [Nelson Lakes](https://abigailhannah.nz/165-an-18-day-mission-through-nelson-lakes-kahurangi-and-abel-tasman-with-the-national-park-trifecta/) was about an hour away. Outside Aoraki and the Mackenzie, it's the first place that made her fall in love with travel: mountains, a beautiful lake and so many hikes, all easy to get to. She's done lots and lots of hiking there (no mountaineering yet), and it's become her sacred little getaway close to home.

## Angelus Hut via Robert Ridge {#angelus-hut}

[Angelus Hut](https://abigailhannah.nz/039-angelus-hut-dylan-arthur/) is one of Mel's favourites, and she reckons it's the best one you could do in the park.

The first time, before she had a 4WD, she planned it as an overnight with a friend, and it ended up a solo hike: her first big overnight tramp on her own. She was nervous, so she got there super early, revved up and wearing far too many layers, an excited girl ready to see some mountains. She'd given herself seven hours, and expected to arrive around 4 or 5pm. She was at the hut by 3pm, with so much time to kill.

She was the first one there that evening, and it was her first time lighting a hut fire, in a big hut, so she was fluffing about with that for a while. Then she watched the sunset from above the huts. On later trips, she's explored further, along the riverbeds that run from the lake.

To give you some context, my first day along Robert Ridge took me eight hours. I was so shattered I was in bed by 8pm, before the kids staying in the hut. But the sunrise the next morning was phenomenal: not a whisper of wind, and the lake like glass. Some of my favourite photos I've ever taken are from that morning.

Mel has done Angelus twice and attempted it a third time:

* **On the edge of winter**, with enough patchy snow to make it interesting.
* **Summer**, with a group of friends, when the lake was swimmable.
* **After fresh snowfall**, with nobody up there since. She had her alpine gear, but she was solo and didn't feel comfortable, so she turned around halfway. Still beautiful.

Her favourite way in is Robert Ridge, because you're up high and surrounded by mountains the whole way, and she finds being up high exhilarating. You can also come back a different way, down through the bush and the valley.

When I was up there, two women in their sixties had caught a water taxi to the start of their track, walked up, and spent two nights at the hut, with a whole day in the middle to enjoy it. They were winning.

## Parachute Rocks and the Blue Lake plan {#parachute-rocks}

On a spare day, Mel likes to hike up to Parachute Rocks on the St Arnaud Range. It's uphill the whole way, but at the top you get a completely different perspective on Lake Rotoiti. Pop over the top of the range and you get 360-degree views over Nelson and the Wairau Valley, with little alpine tarns on the other side.

Next on her list in Nelson Lakes is the Blue Lake hike, which she describes as five to seven days, zigzagging between a few different huts. She's waiting for a good week of weather, and hopes to do it with a few other people. It starts from Lake Rotoiti, and you can walk the whole way around the lake or take the water taxi to the far end. If you're clever about it, you could detour up to Angelus Hut on the way back. You can't swim in Blue Lake itself.

### Further afield

Mel has seen a lot of New Zealand, but there are still nooks and crannies on her list, especially around Milford Sound: the [Routeburn Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/009-solo-travel-routeburn-track-emily/) and Key Summit, if the hit-and-miss weather down there plays along. The Routeburn is high on my list too.

She hasn't explored the North Island nearly enough. One highlight was a hut hike on Taranaki with a friend. After two days of really average weather, they got to the top and found themselves staring straight at the mountain, completely stoked with the effort it took to get there.

## Tips for hiking solo {#solo-hiking}

Mel's advice if you want to start hiking on your own:

* **Go prepared**, with enough food. She gets snacky, and Jet Planes are a personal favourite.
* **Take something to listen to.** On a longer hike, music or a podcast helps her tune out and get into a flow, though sometimes it's nice to listen to nature.
* **Bring your camera**, which keeps her company and encourages her to the top.
* **Know your limits.** She sometimes doubts herself, and there have been times she's decided not to do something because of her experience level, and would rather have another person with her.

What she loves about hiking solo is that it surprises her every time. At least once on every track she thinks she can't believe she's doing this again, especially when the zigzags keep going round one more corner. Then:

> "once you're at the top you've managed to push through, you're looking at this beautiful view"

You can see where you've come from, and it reminds her how much the human body can do.

## Everest Base Camp and travelling on a budget {#everest-base-camp}

At 18, just under a year after leaving high school, Mel wanted to do something out of the box. She'd just started hiking in Nelson Lakes, Everest Base Camp popped into her head, and she put down a deposit on the trek before she'd booked flights. Now she had to go. It's still one of the best things she's ever done, and she'd go back in a heartbeat.

Her advice if you're thinking about it:

* **Book the trip.** Go with a tour company, and they'll look after you really well.
* **Get fit first**, on some good hikes around New Zealand.
* **Don't doubt yourself.** Altitude is the biggest barrier, so take your time, drink lots of water, take breaks and have acclimatisation days.

She loved Nepal itself: the culture, the people, the scenery and the food.

It was her first overseas trip, and looking back she spent far more than she should have. Now she budgets better and values experiences over things. For visitors to New Zealand, her money-saving tip is to rent a car or a van for a couple of weeks: you're completely mobile, and it's a lot cheaper than trying to stay in hostels, which are harder to find cheaply nowadays.

When I talked to [Goose and Ellen](https://abigailhannah.nz/008-nz-vanlife-gooseandellen/), they put it in a way I'd never heard: a van is your travel and your accommodation in one, so you spend money once instead of twice. I'm the biggest fan of van life, though I know it's not for everyone.

## Photography and shooting the stars {#photography}

Mel's advice for budding photographers is to get out as much as you can. The more you do it, the more you find your own niche and editing style. And:

> "Art is art in my opinion. So I feel like you can't really go wrong as long as you're happy with what you're producing."

She has three lenses. On a big hike she takes them all, but usually she sticks to a wide angle for big landscapes and [astrophotography](https://abigailhannah.nz/027-astrophotography-larryn-rae/), and a portrait lens for more intimate moments.

The night sky is how she fell into photography. With her first little camera, she sat in her bedroom playing with the settings, but it only went up to a 15-second exposure, which isn't quite long enough for stars. The second she worked out how to take those photos, she knew she needed a DSLR. Her favourite place to shoot the stars is Aoraki, a dark sky reserve, with incredible foregrounds and super crisp stars.

My one attempt at astrophotography was in [Tongariro National Park](https://abigailhannah.nz/138-an-obsession-with-the-mountains-of-tongariro-national-park-with-hazel-phillips/), in the middle of winter, before we had our van, when we were sleeping in the back of our station wagon. I got out at 2am, freezing, with no tripod, so I balanced the camera on the car roof. Every photo came out black. Five minutes of frozen fingers later, I worked it out. Lens cap, Abigail.

One of Mel's favourite paid experiences is a scenic flight. [Billy](https://abigailhannah.nz/001-helicopter-over-aoraki-with-billy-nunweek/) took her on his doors-off flight, one of the highlights of her life so far, flying around the tall mountains of Aoraki. She reckons it depends what you want to see: flights out of [Abel Tasman](https://abigailhannah.nz/026-abel-tasman-kyle-mulinder/) go over the ocean, and from there you can see Mount Taranaki in the distance.

## Mel's tips for visiting Nelson Lakes {#nelson-lakes-tips}

Mel's advice for a first visit to Nelson Lakes:

* **Take your togs.** You'll probably end up on a hike, and you should do at least one.
* **Start with Mount Robert.** If you're a beginner, take a whole day to do the loop around the mountain, stop at Bushline Hut for the views and a cup of coffee, then come down for a swim.
* **Stay for sunset**, and if it's a clear night, wait for the stars.

As she puts it:

> "having a Lake dip after a big hike is just so wholesome"
