I've wanted Neha on the podcast ever since she shared this story on her Instagram stories. It was quite a while ago now, and it has stuck with me the whole time. So I asked her to tell it again, right from the beginning, for everyone to hear.

Neha was born in India and grew up in Mumbai, then moved to Christchurch when she was 15. Since then she's lived and worked all over the world, and travel has always been a part of her life. She's a photographer and a bit of a writer, and she's a big fan of a solo road trip.

And this one is soooo good. A week alone in her little hatchback, a heli-hike, one of her favourite sunsets ever, on an empty beach, and a five minute chat on the Heaphy Track that turned into a whole day out with a local farmer (this is the story that stuck with me). So we cover her full West Coast itinerary, the moment she realised she couldn't change a tyre, her tips for a first visit, and where she wants to go next.

## From Mumbai to Christchurch and beyond {#neha-story}

Neha was born in India and grew up in Mumbai. She moved to Christchurch when she was 15, which she calls wild: moving across the world, with three years of high school still to go.

She went to university in Christchurch. Since then she's lived and worked in Mumbai, Dubai and London, and when we recorded, she was living in Sydney.

Travel has always been part of her life. She did her first trip around the South Island in that first summer after the move, seeing everything for the first time, and she's kept it going ever since. She's listened to most of the podcast, so it was her first time on this side of it.

## Fitting travel around work {#travel-and-work}

How Neha fits travel around work has looked different in every country she's lived in.

* **New Zealand:** longer breaks. She loves a road trip, taking her time and stopping wherever she wants, so she'd take a couple of weeks off, get in the car and drive.
* **London:** quick city breaks to Europe. You could book a Ryanair flight for £10, leave on a Friday night (or catch the red-eye 4am flight) and be in a small capital city somewhere in Europe. She did that for a couple of years, and you could basically do a new country every other weekend.
* **Sydney:** day trips up north when she can.

Wherever she is, she carves out time for it, because it makes her happy.

## Planning a solo West Coast road trip {#planning-the-trip}

Neha was between moving from London to Australia when COVID happened, so she had to come home to New Zealand, because she couldn't get in anywhere else. While she was hanging around, she realised it was the perfect time to see a part of the country she'd never been to.

A friend had been to Karamea and came back raving about it. So Neha decided to see it for herself before she moved to Australia, and if she was driving all the way across to the West Coast in her little hatchback, she was going to see as much of it as she could.

The West Coast is her favourite part of New Zealand. You have glaciers, rainforests, ancient caves and the ocean all there together, and she'd find herself going, how is this even real?

She calls herself a bit of a nerd when it comes to researching places. She looks at Google Maps and checks angles, because she does [photography](https://abigailhannah.nz/landscape-photography-meghan-maloney/) and wants to be in the right place at the right time for the sunset. The more she looked, the more she found:

> "It's just fascinating to see what we have on offer in our backyard."

So she booked her stays for the week. Coming from Christchurch, you drive across to the coast and then backtrack, which takes up time. It's such a beautiful part of the country that Neha didn't mind one bit.

### Where is Karamea?

Karamea is the last little town on the West Coast of the South Island, where the road basically ends. Kōhaihai, just north of it, is really the end of the road, but Karamea is the last bigger town. There's a resort there called the Last Resort.

It's isolated, it has its own little microclimate, and everyone seems to know everyone. That friendliness is one of the things Neha loves about small-town New Zealand.

I'd been looking at walking the [Heaphy Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/133-tramping-the-heaphy-track-from-karamea-to-collingwood-with-jacq-nesbit/) from Karamea over to Collingwood in Golden Bay when we recorded, but all the huts were booked for my dates. So I was doing the [Old Ghost Road](https://abigailhannah.nz/solo-old-ghost-road/) instead, in the same region.

## West Coast road trip itinerary {#itinerary}

Here's how Neha's week went, all solo in her little car.

### Christchurch to Arthur's Pass

Any time Neha drives to the West Coast, she always stops at [Arthur's Pass](https://abigailhannah.nz/153-arthurs-pass-and-three-attempts-at-mount-philistine-with-amelia-hayes/) to have a coffee and walk around. If you have the energy, go up to the Devil's Punchbowl Falls. The falls are beautiful, and the stairs can kill you. She grabbed a pie and carried on.

### South to Franz Josef

The glaciers were a bucket list item for Neha, so she headed south to Franz Josef first. She stayed a couple of nights, did a heli-hike and visited Lake Matheson.

### Franz Josef to Karamea

The drive from Franz Josef up to Karamea was her longest day of driving, and a very, very long one. If you've driven in New Zealand, you know you can easily add a couple of hours to whatever Google Maps says, because you stop so many times to take photos. She parked herself in Karamea for three nights.

### Punakaiki and home via Lewis Pass

On the way back towards Christchurch she stayed a couple of nights in Punakaiki. Then she came home via Lewis Pass, which was very cool: a different road with different townships.

## Franz Josef heli-hike and an Ōkārito sunset {#heli-hike-okarito}

When you travel, some days stick out, and there are moments when you look at the scene in front of you and pinch yourself. For Neha, they make her feel so grateful for what we have in our backyard.

### The heli-hike and the ice caves

Flying to a glacier in a helicopter and then walking on it was amazing. They crawled through ice caves and made their own path across the ice, then she'd look up and see a waterfall and rainforest. You don't get that in many places around the world, and it felt so untouched and pure. She never imagined she would be crawling through an ice cave.

### Sunset at Ōkārito Lagoon

Ōkārito Lagoon and its beach sit just north of Franz Josef. As a photographer, Neha loves sunrises and sunsets, and growing up in Christchurch and living in Sydney, she doesn't get many sunsets over the ocean. So she made sure she was on a beach.

The whole beach was bathed in golden light. There wasn't another soul there, only Neha on a swing, watching the sun go down by herself, with the waves crashing and the ruggedness of the West Coast all around. It made her feel almost insignificant, and so grateful. She still gets goosebumps talking about it, and it remains one of her favourite sunsets, and she's seen a lot of them.

I love that in New Zealand you can have a whole beach to yourself, especially on the West Coast, where so few people go. If you weren't there that evening, the sky would still be putting on that show for nobody.

## Karamea, the Ōpārara Arches and a Kiwi grandfather {#kiwi-grandfather}

This is Neha's favourite moment of the whole trip. She says it doesn't happen very often, and it's such a classic New Zealand story.

### A chat on the Heaphy Track

While she was in Karamea, Neha decided to walk a little bit of the Heaphy Track, from Kōhaihai to Scotts Beach, which isn't a very long walk at all. She parked at the DOC car park, wandered around taking pictures and crossed the beautiful bridge over the river.

There she met an older gentleman. He said g'day, asked where she was from and what she was doing, then asked if she'd been to the Ōpārara Arches. She hadn't. She'd tried to get on a tour, but the dates didn't match up, and her hatchback was never going to make it along the narrow gravel road.

So he told her she couldn't come to Karamea and not see the arches, and invited her to come by his house at nine the next morning so he could drive her there. Neha won't say what colour his house is, because she thinks all of New Zealand would end up there.

### Checking with the locals

She didn't even know his name. She kept chatting, and learnt he'd lived in Karamea his whole life and was a farmer there. She thanked him and said she'd see how she went.

That evening she had dinner at the Last Resort and chatted to the locals. When she told them about the offer, they said he was a legend. The way they spoke about him, he was held in such high regard in the community, so she knew she could trust him.

### A day out to the arches

At nine the next morning, he'd packed morning tea for them both: fruit, a sandwich and a muesli bar. The torches were ready and so was the ute. First they had a cup of tea, and he showed her photos of how the farm looked ten and twenty years ago.

Then he drove her to the arches and took her on the walk, telling her about the ancient caves, the birds, the plants and the forest. Neha likes to hide in bushes to get every angle and composition, and he waited patiently the whole time.

Back at the farm, he made lunch with fresh vegetables from his garden. Neha pulled out carrots, there was beetroot already pickling, and he had his own salad dressing, which he still won't give her the recipe for. He packed her dinner, because she was driving to Punakaiki afterwards, and took her quad biking to the beach to show her around.

As Neha puts it:

> "it's amazing that a 5 minute conversation on a walking track led to now me calling him my kiwi grandfather because that's what he means to me"

She still calls him every few weeks to check in. They fondly remember that day: she tells him about her life growing up in India, and he tells her how much Karamea has and hasn't changed. That experience is a big part of why the West Coast holds such a special place in her heart. It reminded her the world still has good people (she's not saying you should trust every stranger).

I don't think that day would have happened on a trip with friends or in a group. Those one-on-one connections tend to happen when you're travelling solo. And it was great that the locals at the Last Resort knew exactly who she meant, and that other people knew what she was doing.

## Remote roads, rain and solo travel {#remote-roads}

### Rolling with the weather

Neha says you almost have to work with the weather, and in some parts of New Zealand, rain makes it more beautiful. She was on one of the forest walks in Franz Josef when it started raining, and it made everything so lush. She doesn't think there's a shade of green quite like the West Coast green, and the rain brought it out even more. So she doesn't think of the weather as a challenge.

I had a similar moment at Hokitika Gorge, walking through the bush on my own in the rain, listening to it patter on the trees and watching the drops run down the ferns and bounce off the ends. The rain brought the whole bush to life.

### The tyre she couldn't change

Neha's real challenge was something she hadn't thought about. Driving from Franz Josef to Karamea, she suddenly realised how isolated she was, with no reception, a long way from the nearest petrol station and no house around. In that same moment she realised she didn't know how to change a tyre.

Nothing went wrong, thankfully. But it taught her that if she was going to do solo road trips, she needed to be able to change a tyre, and she can now. Even when you're not camping or doing anything crazy, it's the little things you don't think about, and how remote parts of New Zealand can be.

### What solo travel teaches you

For Neha, a big upside of solo travel is learning to be comfortable with yourself, which doesn't always come easily. Even when you're doing things you love, it's still you, with a lot of driving time on your own. And the roads are so beautiful that you have to keep concentrating. Her verdict:

> "I highly recommend solo road trips. You do one and then they stick with you."

## West Coast tips and what's next for Neha {#west-coast-tips}

Neha's tips for a first visit:

* **Allow more time on the road.** Google Maps tells you a distance, and you'll stop far more often than it allows for.
* **Be present.** Forget about wi-fi and posting on Instagram while you're there. Take in the magic of the West Coast.
* **Don't let the weather hold you back.** One of the locals at the Last Resort told her it's either about to rain or raining there, so if you get a sunny day, you're very lucky and should go and buy a lotto ticket.
* **Go!** A lot of people skip the West Coast altogether, and it took Neha 20 years to go. Now she can't stop talking about it.

There are free walks to see the glaciers too, and the arches are free for anyone to visit.

On one of our road trips, we went as far south down the coast as you can go, past Haast. The road kept getting narrower, with moss and shrubs growing across it, until it turned to gravel and ended at a handful of houses. There were a gazillion sandflies, so take insect repellent, plus a spray for inside the car. We've been right to the southern end, and Neha has inspired me to head north of Westport.

### What's next for Neha

There are so many parts of New Zealand Neha still hasn't done. [Taranaki](https://abigailhannah.nz/156-taranaki-three-peaks-traverse-and-supporting-families-in-the-outdoors-with-jenn-julian/) is very high on her list. She wants to do the [Tongariro Alpine Crossing](https://abigailhannah.nz/172-hiking-the-tongariro-alpine-crossing-and-tongariro-northern-circuit-with-ben-boland/), and she'd love to walk the full [Abel Tasman](https://abigailhannah.nz/026-abel-tasman-kyle-mulinder/), having done bits of it.

I love Abel Tasman, and Taranaki is beautiful. She'll love it so much.
