I can say with 100% certainty that Anja is the first Olympian ever on the podcast. And she's probably the first guest with a Wikipedia page too (I did a quick Google before we recorded).

Anja is a halfpipe skier from New Zealand. She grew up in the North Island, found herself wandering down to the South Island, and is a big fan of winter, which is kind of ironic for a summer baby. She competed at the 2022 Beijing Olympics, and when we recorded she was in Wānaka, mid-season, planning her next winter of World Cups overseas.

So we go from her very first trip to the snow (bombs and a prison were involved, as far as six-year-old Anja was concerned) all the way to the Olympic bubble. Then I picked her brain: which New Zealand ski fields are best for learning, where advanced riders should head, and whether I should finally try skiing. Spoiler, she's really really biased.

## Halfpipe skiing and how Anja got started {#anja-story}

### What freestyle halfpipe skiing is

Freestyle skiing covers a wide variety of events: slopestyle, big air, halfpipe, aerials, moguls and more. Halfpipe is one small category inside it, and Anja does it on skis.

Her easiest way to explain it is to name someone people already know, like Shaun White or Tony Hawk. Once they've pictured the skateboarder, she puts it like this:

> "okay you know that like big tube thing that he like rides on a skateboard that's like made of wood. Yeah I do that."

Hers is on skis, it's longer, and it's built out of snow.

### A first trip to the snow at six

Anja was six when she started skiing. It came about through [surf life saving](https://abigailhannah.nz/112-crossing-the-cook-strait-on-a-paddleboard-and-surf-lifesaving-with-britt-spencer/): family friends from the club went skiing in winter, so the kids could hang out in the winter as well as the summer.

The first trip set the tone. A wrong turn in the dark took them through Waiouru, which to six-year-old Anja meant land mines on the side of the road, and then past a low-security prison, which she assumed the prisoners were about to escape from. So her introduction to everyone at the ski club was running up the stairs announcing she'd been taken to a place with bombs and a prison. She decided she liked this: cold, and an adventure.

After that it was a few weekends a year. At school she joined the ski team, mostly as a way to get another team jacket, and did some racing. She was not good at it, she says.

### Moving to Wānaka at 16

On a trip to [Wānaka](https://abigailhannah.nz/154-working-as-a-sky-diving-instructor-in-wnaka-with-anne-bonfert/), sitting in a cafe, Anja decided she was moving there the next year. She was 16 and about to be in Year 12, so moving wasn't exactly the plan. She made a PowerPoint presentation, and she ended up moving down.

That's how halfpipe started. She made friends with people who were trying to qualify for the 2018 Olympics, they kept inviting her into the halfpipe and onto the slopestyle course, and she got hooked. None of it was intentional.

## From Mammoth to the Beijing Olympics {#road-to-beijing}

### An injury, then a pandemic

Anja's first World Cup was at Mammoth in California in 2019, just before she turned 20. Shortly before it, while training in Switzerland, she injured her ankle. She competed anyway, then found out at home she'd injured it pretty badly, and took the overseas winter off.

Cleared to ski on 8 March 2020, she headed overseas and had four days skiing at Copper Mountain in Colorado before her roommate told her the mountain was closing for fourteen days. Everyone knew it wouldn't be fourteen days. She got a flight home, the last one, she says, that let tourists into the country, and she was able to isolate at home.

The New Zealand season that followed wasn't much of a halfpipe season, because the usual pipe shaper couldn't come to New Zealand.

### Nine months away and an accidental Guatemala trip

Next came nine months overseas, between the States and Europe. While she was in Europe, a rule came in stopping travel from Europe to the States for anyone who wasn't a US citizen, so she had to quarantine somewhere else for two weeks first. She picked Guatemala and loved it, even as the odd one out travelling through the airport with a ski bag.

> "Everybody was coming up and opening my bag and asking questions. It was fascinating."

Then it was back to New Zealand, and overseas again for a winter chasing World Cup results to qualify for the Games. She qualified.

### Skiing like the Michelin Man

The changes in temperature were brutal. At the Calgary qualifier, her goggles would freeze up after two runs, so she'd go back inside, take her boots off, defrost, then go and do another two runs. Brutal at the time, kind of funny now.

The cold does affect performance. Her boots get brittle, her joints get sore (she's still dealing with an annoying ankle), and there's no way to warm up. So you wear as many layers as possible and go up the wall to spin like the Michelin Man.

I'm a pretty cold person, the one wearing five layers on a normal day, so I loved that image.

### Inside the Beijing bubble

The lead-up to the 2022 Beijing Olympics meant a lot of COVID tests. China was still locked down, so coaches and athletes from different countries flew in on a charter flight, spaced out so nobody would become a close contact.

If you followed the Games, you'll have seen the hazmat suits. By then, Anja says, it had got to the point of being funny. The suits had the Olympic logo on them, and getting your hands on one became a bit of a game. She didn't get one. The thing she found more apocalyptic was the roads being closed so the buses could go through.

Her team missed the opening ceremony, because you fly in for your own part of the Games, but halfpipe was one of the last events, so they stayed for the closing ceremony. Everything was contained to keep people safe, so it felt like its own little world, going quickly and in slow motion at once.

## Life as a halfpipe skier at Cardrona {#life-at-cardrona}

At the start of the season Anja was working five to six days a week in food and beverage. When we recorded, she was basically a full-time athlete.

Work is tricky because she's so dependent on the weather: rostered on for the only bluebird day of the week, she misses it. So she tells her bosses upfront that once the pipe opens, she needs to ski, and on bad weather days she'll happily cover a shift.

She has an awesome bunch of mates she met through working at Cardrona, and her two best mates are her coach and a teammate. There's a whole crew around the mountain who will help out with anything.

Her pick for favourite field is Cardrona, because she rides halfpipe and it's the only halfpipe in the Southern Hemisphere.

## Favourite places to ski overseas {#skiing-overseas}

Anja is still ticking places off her list, but two stand out.

* **Laax, Switzerland.** For her it feels like spring break: even training, she's skiing slushy snow with her mates, away from competition. It's a huge, varied mountain, with powder, freeride and slopestyle, covering three towns.
* **Cervinia, Italy.** It borders Zermatt, so you can ski up to the top and cross between the two countries. She ate focaccia skiing under the Matterhorn from the Italian side, which is the cheaper side, because it's on the euro.

## New Zealand ski fields for learners and experts {#nz-ski-fields}

I asked Anja if she'd been to [every ski field](https://abigailhannah.nz/117-bikepacking-to-all-23-new-zealand-ski-fields-with-tara-and-naomi/) in Aotearoa. Not yet: she has a list.

In the North Island she's skied Whakapapa and Tūroa, and she still wants to ski Tukino, a club mountain. In the South Island she's done the Remarkables, Coronet Peak, Cardrona and Treble Cone. Still on the list are Ōhau, Mount Dobson, Rainbow near Nelson, and the club fields around [Arthur's Pass](https://abigailhannah.nz/153-arthurs-pass-and-three-attempts-at-mount-philistine-with-amelia-hayes/), like Craigieburn and Cheeseman. She drove through Arthur's Pass once and they look incredible.

My first time on the snow was snowboarding at Happy Valley at Whakapapa, because snowboarding seemed so much cooler. I've never skied, and never snowboarded in the South Island.

### Where to learn

For learning to ski or snowboard in the South Island, Anja rates Cardrona and Coronet Peak. Cardrona also has awesome terrain you wouldn't expect, so there's plenty to explore once you've found your feet.

### Where advanced riders should go

* **The Remarkables or Treble Cone** for more advanced terrain.
* **Heli-skiing.** If you're an advanced rider, put a bit of money aside for it.
* **Ski touring.** Get a good crew together, or a guide, and go [ski touring](https://abigailhannah.nz/ski-touring-tasman-glacier/). It gets you off the mountains and away from lift queues.
* **A club field road trip.** Get a [campervan](https://abigailhannah.nz/121-avoiding-vanlife-mistakes-and-life-on-the-road-in-nz-with-tiff-and-nathan/) or a car and do a tour of the club fields and the normal resorts.

Club fields are smaller, with lodges and the like. A few want you to get in touch first, but at a lot of them you can buy a pass and turn up. And if you're newer, like me, you can still go: some club fields have areas where you can learn or get better.

### Anja's first heli-ski

When we recorded, Anja was going heli-skiing for the first time that Saturday. She'd put money aside a while ago, and with this being one of the South Island's best winters, this was the time. Whether they'd leave from Wānaka or [Queenstown](https://abigailhannah.nz/189-moving-to-aotearoa-from-chicago-and-building-a-life-in-queenstown-with-viktoria-de-dominicis/), and which range, was a surprise. After planning the rest of her season herself, she was very happy for somebody else to take the reins for a day of powder turns, then back in the pipe the next day.

## Cross-country skiing at Snow Farm {#cross-country-skiing}

Cross-country skiing terrifies Anja. The first time she went ski touring, with her heels free, she fell on her face so much. Cross-country skis are the same idea, only way skinnier and with no edges. She's keen and terrified at once.

What she's heard is to get a bunch of mates together and head up to the huts around Snow Farm, say for a birthday party. Apparently it's hilarious: the pros glide past while everyone else falls everywhere. With others, it's fine.

> "as long as you get a good story out of the end then you know everything's worth it."

I want to learn to ski because I want to be able to go cross-country skiing, so I was a bit nervous after hearing that.

## South Island ski season and chasing winter {#ski-season}

When we recorded, in September, the mountain had opened in June and was due to close sometime in October. I know it changes every year depending on how much snow there is.

Anja's overseas winter follows the World Cup schedule: the States in December, then Canada and Europe. Whether she goes to camps overseas before that comes down to budgeting.

Anja chases winter around the world. Her last proper summer, she says, was skiing on glaciers at Mount Hood in June, during a heat wave. A two-week trip to New Caledonia definitely counted as summer too.

## Skiing or snowboarding, and getting uncomfortable {#ski-or-snowboard}

Anja's biggest advice: if you want to give skiing a go, go to your nearest resort. It's pretty accessible. Get a pair of skis and try it, because that's what she did, she fell in love with it, and here she is. If you want tips, you can message her on Instagram.

### Can she snowboard?

No. On swap days she penguin slides down the mountain. She can [surf](https://abigailhannah.nz/learn-to-surf-new-zealand/) and she can skate, but toe edges don't make sense to her. Skiing feels like walking or riding a bike, with natural movements. Strapped to a snowboard off a jump, she'd end up on her bum.

### Should I try skiing?

I asked if I should try skiing or stick with snowboarding (wrong person to ask, I know). Her answer: you can't knock it until you try it. You'll like snowboarding more, find you can do both, or like the other one better. Definitely skiing, she says.

### Comfortable with the uncomfortable

When I said she'd scared me off cross-country skiing, Anja was quick to say not to take it out of fear: she was making fun of herself. She's completely fine in a halfpipe, but she's terrified of heights, which a lot of people find odd. You're always going to be scared of something you're not good at, because you're outside your comfort zone.

> "you've got to learn to be comfortable with the uncomfortable so trying something and being scared and laughing at yourself is the only way to get growth from … whatever you want to do and also just in life in general."

So give it a go, get uncomfortable, and laugh at yourself. In ten years you'll either be able to do it, or you'll have the funniest memory.
