Every time I'm in Christchurch I end up asking the same question. What is there to do here? I either suck at researching or nobody is talking about these places. So I was really really excited to ask someone who lives there.

Jasmine Trafford lives in Christchurch with her two little kids, and they get outdoors as much as they can while living in a city. You might know her as Kiwi Kids Outdoors on Instagram. She grew up on a kayak company at the gateway to the [Abel Tasman](https://abigailhannah.nz/026-abel-tasman-kyle-mulinder/), which sounds like a dream childhood to me.

And she's soooo open about how motherhood changed her plans, and how she built back up one small walk at a time. (I was frantically writing notes the whole way through.)

So this post covers her favourite short walks and beaches around Christchurch, what goes in her day pack, a night in the hut on Quail Island, how she finds family-friendly tracks, and the meetups she runs for parents.

## Growing up in Marahau and the Abel Tasman {#marahau-childhood}

Jasmine grew up in Marahau, the little village at the gateway to the Abel Tasman National Park. It's sleepy in winter and absolutely transformed over summer.

Her family set up one of the first kayak companies there, so she and her older brother grew up mucking about as the tourists came and went. She had no idea how lucky she was until she moved away.

[Sea kayaking](https://abigailhannah.nz/114-sea-kayaking-for-beginners-and-the-best-of-the-hauraki-gulf-with-bennett-lim/) was one of their main adventures. The double kayaks had custom-made fibreglass kids' seats that popped into the gear hatches, so two kids rode along with all the gear stashed around them. Jasmine remembers shouting instructions from the front, and getting washed under by the wake of passing boats.

There were plenty of camping trips up the Abel Tasman, and marine life she took for granted: orcas every time they came past, dolphins all the time, and calls from tourist operators they knew to say the whales were out in the bay.

They still go back a few times a year, and showing her own children around the place she grew up is so special.

It's such a beautiful part of Aotearoa. A sea kayak trip there was on our bucket list for years, and when we finally got there, it blew my expectations away.

## Becoming a mum and starting small outdoors {#starting-small}

Before kids, Jasmine packed her week full. She was paddling waka ama on Lyttelton Harbour at least three times a week, going for day hikes and doing lots of short walks on the Port Hills. She'd finish work and squeeze in an adventure before the sun went down.

So she thought she'd be getting outdoors with her baby from day dot. Her plan was to spend maternity leave on the Port Hills, tramping and staying in huts midweek while her partner was working. The transition to motherhood turned out to be big and overwhelming, and her baby wasn't flexible. In that first year, she needed to slow down, and it took her a long time to be okay with that.

They started small: little walks, the beach. It wasn't until her first was over a year old that they started doing overnights, including the Abel Tasman with friends. Since then they've mostly focused on day trips, because that's what works for her particular kids.

Her advice for new parents is to tune in to yourself and your own baby. Every kid is wired differently and responds differently to being away from home. Take inspiration from others, then choose the adventures that will fill your cup in that phase of life. If your baby is unsettled and it's getting frustrating, pare it back to little walks, get used to the gear and the carriers, and build up slowly.

That after-work culture is so Christchurch. When I was in corporate and worked out of the Christchurch office for a couple of days, everyone was heading off on their mountain bikes after work. My Auckland workmates were always surprised when I went tramping.

## Short walks on the Port Hills and Mount Thomas {#port-hills-walks}

Jasmine reckons Canterbury families are spoilt for choice, and the Port Hills are a favourite because they're so close: you can be there within half an hour. She hasn't come close to doing every walk on them.

* **Mount Vernon Valley Track.** It runs parallel to the popular Rāpaki Track and is a lot quieter. There's a little shelter and rocks to scramble on, which older kids love because they enjoy a challenge.
* **Sign of the Bellbird.** Tracks run from here along the crater rim, and a few metres in you're surrounded by korimako (bellbird) song. You get 360-degree views around the city, out to the ocean and out to the harbour.
* **Mount Thomas Conservation Area.** Jasmine loves, loves, loves it for the beech trees and the birdlife. There's a whole smorgasbord of walks, from short ones right through to bigger backcountry tracks.

Beyond the popular spots, every direction you drive brings you to a new forest park or conservation area.

## Christchurch beaches for kids {#christchurch-beaches}

The family lives close to Waimairi Beach, so they're down there all the time. Two more of Jasmine's favourites:

* **Taylors Mistake.** A big surf beach with rock pools to explore and rocks to clamber over, plus plenty of places for kids to potter.
* **Tumbledown Bay.** A real gem over the hill past Little River. The gravel road along the cliff feels a little dodgy, then it's a really short walk down. They saw seals, people surfing and fishing, and little pools beside the rocks for swimming, plus a stream for building dams and driftwood huts.

## Day hike essentials with kids {#day-hike-essentials}

Jasmine always takes a [personal locator beacon](https://abigailhannah.nz/plb-safety/), or in their case a messaging device that also works as a beacon. Short walks around Christchurch are often out of reception. Her reasoning: whether you've walked 500 metres or 20 kilometres, if you get injured, your kids can't go and get help, especially when they're very young.

She knows this one first hand. They don't take a beacon to playgrounds, but one day at Spencer Park, which you can drive straight into, she slipped on matting covered in moss at the end of winter and broke her ankle, in three places as it turned out. She was there alone with her two little kids, in a spot with no reception, and her phone was on 10%. She managed a quick call to her partner, two ambulances came, and her eldest had to go and get help. That's why they take a beacon anywhere with little kids.

The rest of her day pack:

* **The drive, planned like a road trip.** A little entertainment and a few snacks in the car, so everyone arrives happy and ready to get cracking. On the track itself there's so much to see that the kids need nothing else.
* **Lots of water.** The kids love drinking from the hydration pack, so it disappears fast, and she keeps a spare bottle stashed in the bottom of her bag.
* **A first aid kit.** Bumps and bruises happen, and it keeps everyone happy.
* **Layers**, especially in winter. Comfortable kids are happier kids.

Accidents can happen anywhere, at the playground or in the backcountry. We usually only hire a beacon for multi-days, so maybe this is my sign to sort out some communication for day walks too.

## Lake Brunner and a night on Quail Island {#quail-island-overnight}

The family goes through patches: lots of weekends away in a row, then home for ages.

### Lake Brunner

Jasmine loved driving to Lake Brunner, only three hours from Christchurch. They booked a beautiful Airbnb with an outdoor bathtub, swings, hammocks, native forest and a little stream running through the property.

### Quail Island, from Lyttelton Harbour

Jasmine loves a small adventure, and Quail Island is so accessible. You start at Lyttelton Harbour and catch the Black Cat cruise boat across. Her little boy, obsessed with trucks and machinery, was having an adventure before they'd even left.

The island is special to Jasmine because she's paddled over to it in the waka lots of times, and she really wanted to take her boy. This was before her second child was born, and they went in winter with family friends and their kids. Winter is really achievable there, and the hut isn't booked out as often.

The hut is a short wander uphill from the boat, perched on the side of the island looking out across the water. It has two bedrooms, which was key for her son's temperament. There's a fireplace, and he loved trekking to the shed for wood, bringing it in and doing all the jobs to get the fire going. They mucked around in the bush, ate dinner outside and watched the sunset.

It was all going really well until bedtime. They co-sleep, which is handy on overnights, so the two of them shared a lower bunk. But he got more and more unsettled through the night, and by morning she couldn't settle him. They set off on the walk around the island, which runs up high on the tops. He walked a little, then wanted to stay in the pack, so Jasmine charged round the whole circuit and back to the hut while their friends enjoyed it at a gentler pace. Back in Christchurch, it turned out he was unwell.

She'd still go again, and the way she sees it:

> "Even when things don't go well, you often get back and still think, I'm still so glad we did this."

Quail Island has beaches to stop and play at, shipwrecks to look at down at the bottom of the island, and you can camp in a tent if your kid does better in a tent than a hut. You can also take your own boat or kayak across. Jasmine has paddled around it many times in the waka, finding little caves and, if you're lucky, dolphins.

## Finding family-friendly tracks {#finding-tracks}

Jasmine's starting point is DOC. She prints out the DOC PDFs, highlights them and makes notes on what would suit kids and what might be a challenge. In her spare time, she reads DOC brochures.

Her other tools:

* **Reviews** on AllTrails, taken lightly. They're a guide, and the official information is what to trust.
* **Other outdoorsy people.** They love sharing tips and locations.
* **Going there.** Once you're there, you can see what else is around, and every short walk gives you a list of the next ones to notch up the challenge.
* **Google Maps on satellite view**, zoomed in, to see what's really there.
* **Social media**, where so many people share ideas.

On that last one, I was once asked on someone else's podcast about the downside of [travel influencers](https://abigailhannah.nz/180-hiking-the-cascade-saddle-route-tramping-clubs-and-influencers-with-alice-milne/). My answer is that you see the picture-perfect moment on top of the hill without seeing what it took to get there. Find the inspiration on Instagram, and do your research outside it.

Jasmine adds that what you're seeing could be years of building up with children. They might have started with a 10-minute walk to the beach, then half an hour, then a little further. She has two opposite children, so she knows how different that can be. Kids have good and bad days, the same as adults:

> "One day they might make it to the trig of a mountain and other days they might not. And that's okay."

### Ages and stages

With her first, Jasmine found it challenging for about the first four years. With her second, she could have been living in the mountains in a tent. With the kids who need more scaffolding, the reward is huge once you start doing bigger things, because you kept trying the little adventures.

On the usual trajectory, a newborn who happily sits in a pack is easy to wander anywhere with, once your body has had time to recover. When they start walking, they often want to walk, so your adventures shrink for a while, change shape or take longer. Jasmine likes that, because it slows you down:

> "when you've got a little kid who's just learning to walk and just wants to do the walking themselves … you get to … slow down and look at all the little mushrooms and the flowers"

Then come the big emotions of the toddler years. Now her eldest is so keen to go and do things, especially one-on-one, and that time feels easy.

## Kiwi Kids Outdoors meetups in Christchurch {#kiwi-kids-outdoors}

A listener in Christchurch asked where to find other mums to get outdoors with. Jasmine's first point: being outdoorsy before kids doesn't make it easy after, and a community of parents in a similar phase helps.

Through Kiwi Kids Outdoors, the passion project she started just over a year before we recorded, she runs a fortnightly hiking group for parents. It's open to mums, dads, caregivers, anyone with children five and under, plus older children who are homeschooled. She wants it to be as inclusive as possible. Jasmine is clear that it's a meet-up, without a guide. She often scopes the tracks out first as a bit of solo time, then sends out the details and the hazards for kids.

They've since added a nature play group alongside it, for parents who are pregnant again or juggling two ages. One group stays at the start for open play outdoors. A parent-paced group walks further with babies in packs, and a slower group of walking toddlers does a shorter loop. If someone wants to turn back, someone goes with them.
