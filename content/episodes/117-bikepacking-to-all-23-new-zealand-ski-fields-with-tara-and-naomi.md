Today's chat is with Tara and Naomi, who call themselves biker number one and biker number two. And their mission was a big one: [bikepacking](https://abigailhannah.nz/beginner-bikepacking/) to every ski field in New Zealand, in winter, skiing at almost every one along the way.

Tara is originally from Canada and has lived here on and off for years. Naomi is a Kiwi who grew up in Hamilton and lives in Christchurch. Neither of them was a cyclist before this trip. Tara didn't even really own a bike. So they became cyclists in about a year (I love that so much).

I've done a bit of snowboarding but I've never skied, and by the end of this one I had a list of club fields written down. We go through the route from Taranaki down to the last climb up Treble Cone, the big days and the night rides, the chest infection that took Naomi off the bikes for a while, their favourite clubbies, and what they'd tell anyone planning something similar.

## How Tara and Naomi got into the outdoors {#how-they-started}

Tara reckons she wasn't very outdoorsy as a kid, but she grew up camping in Canada and spent heaps of time outside in the country. When she moved to the city for high school in Montreal, she was always the friend saying let's go camping, to people who'd often never been. She fell in love with the outdoors young, then got really into skiing at university.

Naomi didn't grow up outdoorsy, or even sporty. She was the kid always picked last for sports teams at school, and she describes herself as extremely uncoordinated. She found horses instead, spent her weekends volunteering with them, and dreamed of living on a horse ranch like McLeod's Daughters.

It was at uni that she fell in love with the outdoors. She discovered surfing, and she and a friend started a blog about hikes around the North Island, hoping to get their uni friends into it.

I'm definitely that friend sometimes too: let's go on a hike, let's go camping.

## The idea to bikepack every ski field {#the-idea}

The idea was Tara's. She'd come home from a big ski trip in India and was in that post-trip come-down. Then she met a woman at a ski field who had biked from Christchurch to Mount Olympus. Tara thought it was such a cool idea, and immediately wondered: what if we did all of them?

She went looking for a friend to do it with, and Naomi was the obvious choice. They've adventured together since both moving to Christchurch, and Tara describes Naomi as a boss woman who's always done endurance things.

Naomi was on board right away. They were on a chairlift going up Mount Hutt at the time. At first they talked about just the South Island, because Naomi thought three weeks off work was probably all she could get. Then it grew into the whole of New Zealand. She'd never done any bikepacking, but it sounded like a great adventure, and she was keen to check out all the fields.

### Getting the time off

The trip took 46 days. Naomi works as a nurse, and seven weeks of annual leave is pretty much unheard of. She was ready to resign if she had to. Then a sign went up at handover one day saying leave was available over the exact months they were going, so it worked out really well.

Tara was doing her PhD, and her supervisor was supportive from the start, so getting the time was no problem for her.

## Planning a winter bikepacking trip {#planning-logistics}

Neither of them had done anything this big before, so they planned every single day. First came the route, then where to sleep in between. In winter, a lot of little towns have their hotels closed, which means extra kilometres. Working out where they could ride safely took a lot of logistics, and a pretty intense spreadsheet.

They'd estimated about 45 days, so 46 was pretty bang on, but the schedule was very tight. Naomi thinks they planned it a bit like road cyclists, forgetting how much weight was on their bikes and how much else they'd be doing in a day, like skiing.

Then the weather had its say. They were stuck in Kimbell for three days because it was too windy and everything was shut, and they still had to be places on set days, so the plan changed daily around the forecast.

### How much skiing they fitted in

Most days they had an hour or two of skiing. They got their skis on at every field except Tukino, where they carried their skis up but the snow line was high and the weather wasn't good enough to hike. Every other field got at least 30 seconds of skiing.

## North Island ski fields, Taranaki to Ruapehu {#north-island-route}

They started at Manganui on [Mount Taranaki](https://abigailhannah.nz/156-taranaki-three-peaks-traverse-and-supporting-families-in-the-outdoors-with-jenn-julian/), which neither of them had been to. Naomi calls it an elusive wee ski field, because it's rare for it to get a good snowfall. They arrived in torrential rain, and overnight there was a little dusting of snow.

From there they rode the Forgotten World Highway, which Naomi hadn't heard of before the trip: a beautiful road through lush New Zealand rainforest. They stayed at the Whangamōmona Hotel. As you enter town, a sign tells you you're entering the Republic of Whangamōmona, and they met the so-called president at the hotel.

That drive and that exact hotel have been on my list for ages, because you can even get a stamp in your passport.

### Ruapehu and the crater lake

They biked across to [Ruapehu](https://abigailhannah.nz/032-ruapehu-solo-hazel-phillips/) in bluebird weather. Then they skied over the mountain to Tūroa on the other side, with a trip up to the crater at the top. Seeing the crater lake surrounded by snow was a special moment for Naomi.

### Tukino and the first meltdown

From Tūroa they biked to Tukino, a little club field on the back of Ruapehu that probably isn't super well known. Getting there meant the Desert Road, then a climb where the clay was so thick it was like bike glue. It was their first off-road uphill, on day five, and they ended up pushing their bikes for a big part of it.

It was also their first mini meltdown. Tara says it hit them what was in store for the rest of the ski fields, and they had a moment together of wondering if they could do this.

They spent a couple of nights in Ohakune, including a sick day, then rode State Highway 1 most of the way to Wellington, because it was the fastest way. Locals told them some of the gravel routes would be hard on fully loaded bikes. The traffic was a little bit scary, especially the big buses that rocked them on the side of the road. Then they took the ferry across with their bikes.

## South Island ski fields and highlights {#south-island-route}

In the South Island they followed the Alps down: the Rainbow Road, Hanmer, Mount Lyford, the Craigieburn fields, then Tekapo and the Mackenzie, all the way to Queenstown. Tara recommends the Rainbow Road in winter. The weather was great, it wasn't too hot, and it felt like they were the only people out there. Pressed for time and on heavy bikes, they spent a lot of the trip on the side of the road. With a few extra weeks, Tara reckons you could ride tons of trails.

### Blenheim to St Arnaud into a headwind

One of Tara's most memorable days was riding from Blenheim to [St Arnaud](https://abigailhannah.nz/108-fastpacking-from-st-arnaud-to-arthurs-pass-in-8-days-with-alice-brice/) into a steady headwind. It took far longer than they'd planned, and it was the first day they rode as a peloton: five kilometres on the front, five kilometres off, all day. Type two fun, and one of her highlights.

### A night ride to Arthur's Pass

To beat the weather, they rode from Castle Hill to [Arthur's Pass](https://abigailhannah.nz/153-arthurs-pass-and-three-attempts-at-mount-philistine-with-amelia-hayes/) for Temple Basin at night, on purpose, starting at about 6pm. The pass had been shut that day because of a slip, so they were the only people on the road. Tara calls it a magical night and one of the highlights of the trip.

They both enjoyed riding in the dark: quiet roads, a peaceful feel, and big lights on the bikes so every car could see them.

On another day, during a big storm in Canterbury while Naomi was sick, Tara rode a huge distance alone to get over a pass before the storm hit, so she could finally ski fresh snow. She can't describe how pleased she was to reach the top.

### The Mount Dobson powder day

After three days stuck in Kimbell, they had to ride from Kimbell via Mount Dobson all the way to Ōhau Lodge in one day. They started at 5am. And that day, Mount Dobson had insane powder.

They only had a short window on the hill. It turned into one lap, then one more, then a hike up a peak because that area looked really good. They arrived at Ōhau after midnight. Totally worth it.

### Mount Olympus and the Treble Cone finish

Mount Olympus was one of Naomi's favourites before the trip, and it still is. There's a lodge right on the mountain, they were greeted with prosecco at the bottom of the tow, and they had a couple of slower days there. The lodge hosted charades and karaoke nights, the chef made amazing feasts, and some friends came to catch up with them.

For the last day, they joined a big group of ski cyclists who ride from Wānaka town up Treble Cone every year. There were lots of people with skis and splitboards on their bikes in all sorts of setups, all climbing the final mountain together. They had great weather, skinned up to the top of Treble Cone and had champagne at the top. Tara says being surrounded by people who understood the suffering and the joy was the perfect way to end the trip.

A few of my friends did that same ride, and from their stories it looked steep and pretty gnarly.

## The hard parts of bikepacking in winter {#challenges}

Naomi thinks they hadn't trained enough with weight on the bikes. In training she'd once put a five kilo weight on her bike as a test, and the loaded bikes were so much slower than she imagined.

She also came down with something on day one, a sore throat that became a cough over the first two weeks. She kept pushing through, and it turned into a chest infection that took her off the bikes for several days. Coming back from that, her energy was always a struggle on the huge days.

The mental side was big too: 12 hours on the bike in a day, often. The hardest days were the headwinds, crawling along at 10 kilometres an hour with a long way still to go, and a little voice suggesting you give up or stop halfway. But stopping early means adding the distance on somewhere else. Having each other helped. Naomi says Tara is so encouraging, and they'd lift each other up when one was feeling flat, or stop at a nice cafe for something yummy.

Tara agrees you get fit on the bike. Once you know you can do it physically, the mental side is the hard part. After the Ōhau day, they had to get up in the morning and ride up to the ski field. Some mornings she did not want to get on her bike. By the end, though, it was almost meditative:

> "at the end of the day like you just keep pedaling and you just put one foot in front of the other and eventually you get there and you're done"

Neither would repeat it. Tara fell in love with bikepacking and might do something similar somewhere else, and Naomi's next idea is a bikepacking surf mission somewhere warm, with a light board and very few clothes.

## Favourite club ski fields {#club-fields}

Both of them love the clubbies. Tara is into backcountry skiing, so she loved the really small fields. Fox Peak was a first for her, and even without time to go proper backcountry, it left that vibe with her, with a cool community. Awakino is very off the beaten track: a cute little lodge where you can stay, a rope tow, and the feeling that you almost own the place.

Naomi loves Fox Peak and Awakino too, as Kiwi as it gets. A lot of the clubbies were started by farmers who wanted to go skiing. You ride the rope tow with a nutcracker, a piece of metal you clamp around the rope, and the tows run off tractor motors, with one big shed holding a whole tractor with its wheels taken off. The fields are run by volunteers.

In Craigieburn she loves Broken River and Craigieburn itself, where you drive up forest roads with trees covered in snow. The terrain is beautiful and the cultures are warm and welcoming. As Naomi puts it:

> "the clubbies are really special. I think that's a huge, huge reason why we want to do this trip is to explore our amazing New Zealand club fields"

They might sound a bit daunting, she says, but they're something to be experienced.

My snowboarding has mostly been at Whakapapa, and I'd love to learn to ski. I've written down all their favourite club fields.

## Advice and the documentary {#advice}

Tara's advice is that if you have the idea, you're often the only thing stopping yourself. They barely cycled before this, and it ended up being a cycling trip.

> "just start because you know like you don't even know all the steps that you need to take until you start doing it. And then all of a sudden, like one thing after the other, after the other, it all kind of falls into place."

Then trust yourself to make decisions as they come, because however much you plan, you'll have to adapt.

Naomi's version: if you have the idea, do it. Also, a good spreadsheet goes a long way, and Tara is an absolute spreadsheet queen. For a trip this size, Naomi suggests starting to plan a year out. They had the idea a year ahead, but only started planning about six months out, and it became a second full-time job. Be ready to change plans along the way. And for a biking trip, get a great pair of bike shorts with a good chamois, plus chamois cream. Tara reckons the saddles made a big difference too.

When we recorded, they were making a little documentary about the trip with a filmmaker, aiming for film festivals the following year, with a crowdfunder and teaser planned for later in the year. They're excited to share New Zealand's ski communities with the world, and to encourage everyone to go and support the clubbies.
