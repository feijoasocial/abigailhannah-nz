I love it when listeners reach out to come on the show. And Larissa is a long-time listener, so this one was really really special.

Larissa is Australian, she's in her early fifties, and she absolutely adores travelling in New Zealand (she'd love to make it home one day). After a big life change in her late forties, she came over on her own for four months, based herself in four South Island towns and spent most of her time taking photos at sunrise and sunset. Then she went home and had the post-trip blues so so badly.

So we talk about how she slow travelled, her favourite spots in each base, a two-week South Island road trip you could copy, and what it's like travelling solo as a middle-aged woman. Also scones. A lot of scones.

## From rural Victoria to falling for New Zealand {#larissa-story}

Larissa was always a very outdoorsy kid. She grew up in rural Victoria riding horses, and travelled a lot around Australia with her family. When she was about nine, she spent a year out of school camping around Australia with them and doing correspondence schooling. It was a fantastic experience, and decades later, it's had a lasting impact.

Plenty of people told her she had to go to New Zealand, but she doesn't love flying, which could be why she put it off for so long. She made her first visit about eight years before we recorded, fell head over heels in love, and has tried to come back two or three times a year ever since.

When her life changed in her late forties, New Zealand was a no-brainer. It was where she wanted to go to heal and reset. She tried to talk her manager into letting her go for a year, unsuccessfully, so she spent four months here instead, from March to July.

She wasn't nervous about New Zealand itself. It's close to Australia and culturally so familiar. What worried her was losing the routine of work, which had got her through the hardest times, and she thought she might land and fall in a heap. It was the complete opposite:

> "I landed and I just started to thrive, really from day one."

She calls them the most incredible four months of her life.

## Four months of slow travel in the South Island {#four-months-slow-travel}

Larissa loves really settling into a place. Constant travelling isn't for her, so she based herself in four places: Bob's Cove, just out of Queenstown, then Arrowtown, Clyde and Twizel. That gave her time to find her way around, find her favourite coffee shop and scout spots to come back to at sunset or sunrise with her camera.

She stayed almost entirely in Airbnbs, because they gave her a home base where she could cook and do her washing. Every host gave her a discount for staying longer than three weeks. She bought a car before she arrived, and had no interest in a campervan: she wanted a bed and a house.

She didn't work at all while she was here. She switched off completely, didn't even think about work, and only realised once she had the chance how much she needed it.

### Bob's Cove

Larissa walked down to the cove itself every day. Early in the trip, when things back home upset her, she'd get in the car, walk the lovely track through the bush down to the cove and sit there. She found it really grounding. From Bob's Cove she made many, many drives to Glenorchy too.

### Arrowtown

Arrowtown has heaps of tourists, but when they go home, it's such a wonderful little town, with so many great walks up through the gullies. She spent a lot of time exploring and walking along the river and around Lake Hayes.

My first time in Arrowtown, late at night on a long weekend, we waited 45 minutes in a restaurant without anyone taking our order, so we left and I decided I didn't like Arrowtown. I was judging it off one experience. When we went back this winter, it turned out to be such a cool, quaint spot, and now I want to go back and explore those trails.

### Clyde and Twizel

Around Clyde, Larissa loved heading out into the valley, where there are no tourists, only big hills and big sky.

Twizel, she thinks, is a completely underrated town. She loves the Mackenzie Country, and Twizel is surrounded by lakes and mountains, so close to [Mount Cook](https://abigailhannah.nz/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/) and Lake Ōhau.

## A daily routine of sunrise, scones and photography {#daily-routine}

Larissa very quickly found a new routine. Most days she was up at the crack of dawn to [photograph](https://abigailhannah.nz/landscape-photography-meghan-maloney/) the sunrise, if the weather looked okay. Then coffee somewhere, with a scone. Every cafe in New Zealand has scones, and she loves them (or ginger crunch, which she also loves). She became quite the connoisseur.

After that she'd go exploring, driving or walking. She's not a hiker, so her walks run a few hours at most. Afternoons were for photo editing and reading, and she'd pop out again late afternoon for sunset. Her days passed very quickly.

I'm terrible at sticking to a routine (I'm self-employed and every day looks different), but her version sounds like such a good way to do [slow travel](https://abigailhannah.nz/018-slow-travel-mental-wellbeing-pamela/).

## A two-week South Island road trip {#two-week-road-trip}

Here's Larissa's road trip, which works well if you only have two weeks in the South Island.

* **Staveley**: if you land in Christchurch, head here. She stayed about a week to get into the Ashburton Lakes, which are absolutely gorgeous, and you very rarely see anyone else in there.
* **Arthur's Pass**: people on travel forums often call [Arthur's Pass](https://abigailhannah.nz/153-arthurs-pass-and-three-attempts-at-mount-philistine-with-amelia-hayes/) a place to drive through. Larissa loves it and always stays at least a couple of nights, for the mountains, the many little walks and the misty, magical weather.
* **Ōkārito**: the lagoon on the [West Coast](https://abigailhannah.nz/064-west-coast-road-trip/). It's rained every time she's driven the coast, and it's still gorgeous. Ōkārito seemed to be mostly holiday houses, plus a little store hiring canoes for the lagoon. What she remembers most is the sandflies. They were dreadful, and she's a sandfly magnet.
* **Haast**: there's not much there, but she always stops for a night. The West Coast roads are challenging, windy and wet with lots of one-lane bridges, and driving solo, she finds the concentration tiring even over short distances.
* **Lake Hāwea**: the drive from Haast through [Mount Aspiring National Park](https://abigailhannah.nz/148-a-mountaineering-career-and-climbing-mt-aspiring-with-dom-huxley/) is fantastic, with so many waterfall stops. Wānaka is great, but she loves Lake Hāwea because it's that little bit quieter and less touristy.
* **Queenstown and back**: to make it a loop, go from Queenstown up to Twizel, Tekapo or Mount Cook, then back to Christchurch.

That's pretty similar to the [three-week loop](https://abigailhannah.nz/103-the-ultimate-new-zealand-south-island-road-trip-in-winter-with-barb-and-reg/) we did this winter, with extra bits up to Kaikōura and down into Fiordland. If people have time, I always tell them to do the West Coast, and to take it slow, because of the roads and the weather.

## Solo female travel in New Zealand in your 50s {#solo-travel-tips}

This was Larissa's second solo trip here. The first was two weeks between COVID lockdowns, and when the country locked down again, she had to grab a plane home.

She's an introvert, so she's really comfortable alone. She never once felt lonely, because she was filled with joy the whole time. The odd anxious moment came out on dirt roads with no mobile reception, wondering what would happen if her car broke down. Nothing did.

Her tips:

* **Tell someone your plans.** She did a lot of walks alone, often at the end of a dirt road with no reception, so she always let someone know where she was going and when she'd be back.
* **It's familiar, and also very different.** For Australians, the culture is so similar and there's no language barrier, but the landscape is nothing like Australia. She says it's more like Europe.
* **You'll feel safe.** As a middle-aged woman travelling solo, she never felt unsafe at all, and she finds New Zealanders unfailingly friendly and helpful.
* **Kiwi drivers don't tailgate.** Larissa is a fairly slow driver who always pulls over. Cars here would catch up, back off and wait until she pulled over or there was room to pass. She finds Australian drivers more aggressive, which is quite unpleasant.

I always tell people to pull over too. Our [van](https://abigailhannah.nz/137-a-5-year-journey-from-budget-diy-van-to-dream-home-on-wheels-with-abigail-and-isaac/) climbs hills at turtle speed, so we're always letting people pass.

I think everyone should travel solo at least once. My [first solo hike](https://abigailhannah.nz/solo-old-ghost-road/) was out of necessity, when my husband was off kayaking and I didn't want to sit around waiting, and it was such an incredible experience.

## Post-trip blues and planning the move {#post-trip-blues}

Coming home was the hardest part of the whole trip. Larissa struggled to readjust and to go back to work, even though she loves her job. She cried the whole way home on the plane, and she was really down for a good while afterwards.

What helped was booking another trip very quickly, so she had something to look forward to. It also made her reassess what she wants out of life and where she wants to live. She wants to live in New Zealand, and she's slowly working her way towards it. She says the process is easy for Australians, who are treated much like New Zealand citizens and can move over and buy property straight away.

When we recorded, she was about to fly out for five weeks, her first trip in the lupin season in a long time. She knows lupins are a weed, but they're so beautiful. She planned to spend time in the Mackenzie Country photographing them, then stay in Glenorchy, which she's visited lots but never stayed in. In March she was heading back to stay in Te Anau and drive through the [Catlins](https://abigailhannah.nz/139-slow-travel-in-an-ev-and-catlins-dunedin-and-oamaru-with-mature-flaneur-tim-ward/).

She's never been to the North Island, because the South Island keeps calling her back.

When she's ready, I'll be showing her the [Central Plateau](https://abigailhannah.nz/138-an-obsession-with-the-mountains-of-tongariro-national-park-with-hazel-phillips/) with all its volcanoes, and [Rotorua](https://abigailhannah.nz/038-drone-photography-rotorua-petra-leary/) with its geothermal activity.

Her final advice, especially for anyone going through a big life change, is simple:

> "I think if you're thinking about going, just go, because it is such a wonderful country."

New Zealand isn't super expensive, the roads are generally excellent, and as she puts it:

> "The people are lovely. The scenery is incredible. Just book a ticket and go."

Her recent favourite listen was [Brad Willetts](https://abigailhannah.nz/109-an-epic-4wd-summer-road-trip-at-the-top-of-the-south-with-brad-willetts/) and his four-wheel drive travels, especially the story of his mate's car breaking down up north, because it happens.
