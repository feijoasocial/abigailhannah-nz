I always love seeing Renata's adventures around Auckland, because that's how my own adventures started. The local park. A 20 minute drive to a park in a different suburb. The regional parks. Very mini, and there's so so much out there if you go looking for it.

Renata is a mum who shares her family's adventures online, and her big goal is getting other parents outside with their kids. She's recently started tramping with her daughter too.

But this chat is all about Northland. After lockdown, with the borders still closed, Renata, her partner and their toddler hired a van for ten days and did a big loop of [Northland](https://abigailhannah.nz/176-a-locals-guide-to-northland-and-building-hiking-communities-with-hannah-whittaker/). It went so well that it changed their whole plan for the next year. So we go round it stop by stop: freedom camping on the beach, Paihia, Russell and the Waitangi Treaty Grounds, Cape Reinga, Waipoua Forest and the Kai Iwi Lakes (soooo clear). I throw in a few of my own favourite stops from doing the same loop the other way round. And Renata shares what van life with a toddler was really like.

## Getting kids outside in Auckland {#kids-outside-auckland}

Renata thinks Auckland is so big that people tend to stay in the little area they're used to. Step outside it and there's so much to explore: white sand beaches on the east coast, black sand on the west, bushwalks and volcanoes.

Part of why she shares their [Auckland adventures](https://abigailhannah.nz/123-best-auckland-adventures-for-kids-with-outdoorsy-hijabi-mum-veronika-edali/) is to encourage other parents to get out with their kids and to celebrate their wins, whether that's a local playground or a new beach. When you're a parent, getting out can be really hard.

Auckland has both coasts, so you could watch the sun rise out of the ocean and set into it on the same day. There can't be many places in the world like that.

Renata grew up on the North Shore, near the beach, so nature play after school was simply the lifestyle: free and accessible. Since becoming a parent, she finds it so much easier to parent outside, where her kids are calmer and everyone's happier. As she puts it:

> "I find parenting outdoors so much easier than parenting indoors … if your kids are climbing up the walls remove the walls and it's so true."

## From Camp America to a family van trip {#renata-travels}

After university in Dunedin, Renata had itchy feet. She went to America with Camp America as a [rock climbing](https://abigailhannah.nz/143-intro-to-rock-climbing-and-nzs-best-crags-with-josh-parker/) instructor, moved to London, spent a summer season as a tour guide around Europe, then worked in the travel industry in London for years.

She and her partner had already decided to move home. They were about three weeks into a planned three-month trip through India on the way when they decided to head straight back, and then COVID closed the borders. They were so grateful to spend lockdown here, with beautiful walks and the beach every day.

Afterwards, they wanted to travel New Zealand while there were no tourists, so they booked the Northland van trip. It gave them so much quality family time that by the last day they'd decided: her partner would quit his job, they'd rent out the house and book a one-way flight. They spent five months travelling around Asia and the UK with their daughter. Renata calls Northland the catalyst for a lot more travel as a family.

## Planning a Northland road trip in a van {#planning-the-trip}

They picked up their van at Auckland Airport and had ten days to do a big loop. They were embracing the [van life](https://abigailhannah.nz/121-avoiding-vanlife-mistakes-and-life-on-the-road-in-nz-with-tiff-and-nathan/) idea of no fixed itinerary, counting the nights they had left and asking people for recommendations. With a bit more planning, Renata reckons they'd have fitted in more stops.

For places to stay:

* **CamperMate** for finding freedom camping spots.
* **Google Maps** works too: search freedom camping over the area you're in.
* **Holiday parks** every so often, to plug the van in, charge phones and have a shower.

They stopped in Warkworth on the way up to stretch their legs. Their first night was at a freedom camping spot right on the beach, and they woke to the most incredible sunrise over the ocean. Renata kept telling her partner that people would pay $1,000 a night for a hotel with that view, and here they were for free.

I know that feeling. The week after I finished my corporate job, we freedom camped on the way back from a friend's engagement shoot in Northland, and I lay in bed peeking out as the grey sky started to go orange.

## Paihia, Russell and the Waitangi Treaty Grounds {#paihia-russell-waitangi}

Paihia is probably the place most people think of for a Northland road trip, so they spent a few days there, at the Top 10 holiday park. They'd heard mixed reviews of Paihia and Russell as a base, and Renata is glad they picked Paihia.

### Russell

They took the ferry over to Russell for the day. When we recorded, it wasn't expensive and ran every half an hour. Renata found Russell really educational, from the oldest church to the gravestones of people who signed the treaty.

### Waitangi Treaty Grounds

The tour of the Waitangi Treaty Grounds was one of the most significant moments of their trip. As a Pākehā wanting to be a good treaty partner, without fully understanding all the intricacies, Renata found it very confronting. The guides are locals whose families have lived there for generations, and they tell their personal stories as they show you around. Renata says:

> "It was actually just so authentic and it was very very real."

The best way she can describe it is that you go there and feel it. She tells all her friends they have to go.

When we visited the Treaty Grounds on our Northland road trip, the little museum about the Māori Battalion stood out to me, along with the taonga and the waka. Our guide was connected to both sides of the treaty, which was so cool.

### Hole in the Rock cruise

They also did the Hole in the Rock cruise from Paihia. Renata's favourite part was the long lunch stop, with beach time, lawn games and a cafe. She got seasick and they saw no dolphins, and the cruise was still really good. A crew member told her they'd run four packed sailings a day before COVID. When they went, it was one.

## Mangonui, Cape Reinga and Ahipara {#far-north}

Next was Mangonui, which Renata had never heard of. It's the cutest little seaside town, right on the sea, with a walk along the water. Dinner at an Indian restaurant there was an absolute disaster with a toddler. In the morning they played at Coopers Beach, five minutes down the road, which would have been a beautiful place to freedom camp too.

### Cape Reinga

Then came a big day up to [Cape Reinga](https://abigailhannah.nz/119-three-teens-900km-cape-to-cape-human-powered-journey-with-olly-will-and-thomas/). Renata didn't expect the drive to be so spectacular. From the walk out to the lighthouse you can see two oceans meeting, and they met a man who had [walked the entire length of New Zealand](https://abigailhannah.nz/132-nine-months-of-solitude-walking-northbound-on-te-araroa-with-naomi-arnold/) and had just finished there, on his own, with no welcoming party. It was really quiet.

When I went up on Boxing Day on our own Northland road trip, with the borders still closed, it felt like half the country was there. We'd done the loop the other way, up the west coast first.

Renata calls that time bittersweet: they had places to themselves, and they could see what the missing tourists meant for local people.

### Ahipara and Ninety Mile Beach

From Cape Reinga they drove down to Ahipara, pulling over whenever they liked, with the kitchen and togs right there in the van. They stayed a night at a holiday park and walked out onto Ninety Mile Beach, without trying the van on the sand. They were terrified of getting stuck.

We had to pull someone out of there, so make sure you've got a capable vehicle.

## Waipoua Forest and the kauri {#waipoua-forest}

Her partner had two things he really wanted from the trip: dolphins, and Tāne Mahuta. The dolphins didn't show, and when they got to Waipoua Forest, Tāne Mahuta was closed. Next time, they'll check in advance. Even driving through, the forest was incredible, with kauri on the roadside so massive you can't fathom anything larger.

On our road trip we stopped at the Kauri Museum, which is well signposted. They've got slices of massive kauri with events from history marked on them, so you can almost see history in a slice of wood. The colonial history of felling the trees was hard for me.

Renata finds those sides of history uncomfortable too, and they're still part of our history, so it's good to learn about them. Hopefully it helps protect the kauri that are left.

She'd gone in with low expectations of the rest of Northland, because people rave about the Bay of Islands and she'd heard little else. Every day, her mind was blown.

## Opononi and the Kai Iwi Lakes {#opononi-kai-iwi}

They stopped at Opononi on the west coast too.

At Opononi, we visited Manea Footprints of Kupe (full disclosure, we got a discount). It's a Māori-run centre with an immersive experience that tells the story of Kupe, the first person to discover Aotearoa, through his descendants who still live there. They take you on a bit of a hīkoi and bring the stories to life with drama and songs. It had just opened when we went. I'd recommend doing a proper loop of Northland, because the west coast always feels a bit more untouched.

### Kai Iwi Lakes

Renata had never heard of the Kai Iwi Lakes either, and it's one of the most spectacular places she's ever been: white sand and water so clear they swam in May, cold or not. There's a great camping spot, and when they went, there were only a few other vans. She's been told it gets really busy in summer.

They almost didn't stop. Now she tells everyone, and it's close enough to Auckland to feel doable for a camping trip.

I'm hoping to get up there this summer, because I've only been in the shoulder seasons and I only like swimming when it's super warm. Rarawa Beach, the Karikari Peninsula and Rainbow Falls were other favourites from our trip. Even after ten days, Renata says, the more you see, the more you realise there is to see.

## Van life with a toddler {#van-life-toddler}

Renata calls the trip a very, very positive experience, which is why they went on to travel full time for five months. Everyone they met in Northland was incredibly friendly, and their daughter had the time of her life. The tricky bits were small: timing naps with drives, and putting the bed down in the day and back up again.

She thinks there's a real mental block around travelling with kids, and encourages people to try it anyway:

> "kids are so resilient and adaptable and we were just kind of blown away with how well she did on the trip"

They started the trip anxious and finished it knowing they could do this. And she rates van life as the perfect introduction:

* **Stop whenever you need to**, on the side of the road if you have to.
* **Everything's with you.** If your kid runs into the ocean, the towels and a change of clothes are right there.
* **The kitchen comes too**, so a hungry kid is easy, and there's no day bag to pack.

That's one of the things I love most about van life too.
