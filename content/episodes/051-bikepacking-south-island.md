Rachel came to Aotearoa from Canada straight after university, and it's where she found [bikepacking](https://abigailhannah.nz/beginner-bikepacking/). Her very first trip was a solo loop out of Wellington with a broken water filter and flooded rivers. And she came back grinning.

So this one is basically a love letter to seeing New Zealand by bike. Rachel and I talk through her three weeks from Christchurch to Dunedin (Kiwi kindness, hot tubs, free plums, all of it), the trails she'd pick if you're starting out, and her first bikepacking race, around Banks Peninsula, where the sheep nearly broke her brain at 2am.

Then we get into how she made a life out of seasonal outdoor work, and how painting in the backcountry turned into a monthly illustrated guide. I haven't done a lot of cycling myself (yet!) and by the end I really really wanted to.

## From Canada to guiding in Wellington {#rachel-story}

Rachel is originally from Ontario and lives in Canmore, Alberta. She lived and adventured in New Zealand from 2018 to 2020, straight after university.

She grew up travelling and canoe camping, and first visited New Zealand at seven, for a couple of weeks over Christmas. It stuck in her head as somewhere to come back to. Her parents get the I told you so credit, even if bikepacking and mountaineering make them more nervous every year.

She'd worked for Outward Bound in Canada for quite a few years, and in New Zealand her first job was [sea kayak](https://abigailhannah.nz/114-sea-kayaking-for-beginners-and-the-best-of-the-hauraki-gulf-with-bennett-lim/) guiding in [Wellington](https://abigailhannah.nz/021-wellington-gems-local-travel-tilleigh/), where she learned why sea kayaking is unpopular there: it's super windy.

She often jokes about how similar the two countries are, and once named a cycling magazine article after it:

> "New Zealand feels like if you took Canada … and shrunk it into really vertical skinny islands"

You get coast like Canada's Maritimes, the Southern Alps in place of the Rockies, and a wet, rainforest West Coast like theirs. What Rachel loved here is how close together everything is, which is great for adventures by bike.

I haven't been to Canada yet, but it's high on the list.

## Three weeks bikepacking the South Island {#south-island-trip}

Rachel's first bikepacking trip was a solo loop out of Wellington: a 24-hour epic where her water filter broke, rivers flooded and she forded a couple with her bike on her back. She came back with a huge smile and decided she loved it. After that came a five-day trip over Christmas, then this one, her third ever. Straight into the deep end.

After a couple of months working in the North Island with no big adventures yet, Rachel and a riding partner flew to Christchurch to explore the South Island by bike.

### Arthur's Pass and the West Coast

They went straight over [Arthur's Pass](https://abigailhannah.nz/153-arthurs-pass-and-three-attempts-at-mount-philistine-with-amelia-hayes/). The climb is unrelenting and doesn't end, and Rachel remembers being egged along with gummies. At the top, all excited to eat and camp, a DOC ranger came over and told them to move on. So they kept moving.

Then came the [West Coast](https://abigailhannah.nz/064-west-coast-road-trip/), with torrential downpours and sandflies. They rivalled the Canadian blackflies Rachel grew up with, so she has a lot more respect for Kiwi sandfly complaints now.

Near Blackball, soaked from a rough trail, they found a little B&B online. It had no vacancy, but the woman who ran it saw two wet cyclists and told them to camp on the lawn. She had a tub in the garden heated by a fire underneath, let them jump in, and fed them from her garden. Classic Kiwi kindness.

The scariest bits were on the West Coast road. There's one highway, and even linking as many trails as they could, riding next to RVs in pouring rain, with two inches of clearance, was nerve-wracking. Rachel rewrote song lyrics in her head to keep going.

They had some great nights near Haast, in more downpours, then went over Haast Pass.

### Wānaka, the Alps 2 Ocean and on to Dunedin

They rested for a couple of days with friends in Wānaka, then rode the Alps 2 Ocean Cycle Trail, out of the West Coast rain and in the prime of fruit season. They'd just stuffed their bikes with groceries when a farmer pulled up with a truck full of plums and offered them some, because they looked like they needed more food. You can't turn down free plums, so they stuffed their bib pockets.

From the end of the trail they rode down to Dunedin.

### Winging the logistics

The only fixed points were flights into Christchurch and out of [Dunedin](https://abigailhannah.nz/139-slow-travel-in-an-ev-and-catlins-dunedin-and-oamaru-with-mature-flaneur-tim-ward/). Everything in between was a wing-it: random camping, DOC campsites, and a cheap motel when they needed to dry out. That meant flexibility, and plenty of nights where a place was full, shut, or not where Google Maps said, so they rode another hour.

Between Fox Glacier and Franz Josef Glacier, the DOC campsites were so busy they headed down back roads instead, and found a piece of plywood nailed to a tree, with camping spray painted on it and an arrow. Rachel's reaction:

> "this is either a murder story or it's really wholesome kiwi hospitality again"

It was the second. A man living in a school bus had set up a beautiful, mowed campsite by a river, and came over to say hi. You'll never find spots like that planning in advance.

For Rachel, bikepacking is the perfect balance: it slows you down to see things and meet people, and you still cover the variety of a whole region.

## Best cycle trails for bikepacking in New Zealand {#cycle-trails}

A circuit like hers is awesome for variety and towns. For the biking itself, Rachel reckons the Great Rides, like the [Old Ghost Road](https://abigailhannah.nz/solo-old-ghost-road/), are more worth your time. She also did a circuit through the Rainbow Road and Molesworth: trail and gravel riding, epic open expanses, and no RVs.

Her picks:

* **Alps 2 Ocean Cycle Trail.** Awesome for people new to bikepacking. Campsites all along it, a really clear trail guide website, cute little towns and remote camping. It trends downhill to the ocean and can be done in a few days.
* **West Coast Wilderness Trail.** It ends near [Hokitika](https://abigailhannah.nz/062-three-passes-hike/), and it was a favourite: a purpose-built, amazing trail.

Rachel rates our purpose-built, multi-day cycle trails, with fun corners and berms, as something she hasn't seen anywhere else.

I've seen people whizzing past on bikes between Paeroa and Waihī, through the Karangahake Gorge, many times, and that's the one I'd love to start with.

## Racing Le Petit Brevet around Banks Peninsula {#le-petit-brevet}

The next year, Rachel got into bikepack racing, and found New Zealand's elevation a hell of a place to start. She was staying in Lyttelton when a friend told her about the LPB.

Le Petit Brevet goes around the whole of Banks Peninsula. It's a super small, grassroots race: you meet in a park, whoever shows up races, and whoever crosses the finish line first wins. The clock never stops, so whether you sleep or keep riding is your strategy. Rachel was the only woman at the start line, with about a dozen riders. It has grown a lot since, and added more elevation.

Her core memory is 2am, delirious and descending through thick fog, when eyes started flashing past everywhere. She thought she was hallucinating. It was a field full of sheep, their eyes catching her bike light.

At the bottom of the descent she pulled over for an hour's sleep with a mat and her quilt, set an alarm, and slept straight through it. She woke when a friend in the race whizzed past, chased hard, and found him behind her at the top of a climb: he'd seen her sleeping in the ditch and taken a nap too. Rachel would love to race it again on the new course.

## Seasonal work in the outdoor industry {#outdoor-work}

Outdoor guiding and educating is intensive, so time off between seasons is normal, and that's how Rachel got three weeks free. She later came back to New Zealand for an outdoor education job near Christchurch, and guided for Backroads, a cycling and hiking company, out of Wānaka.

These days she's a whitewater raft guide in Canada, working full on through summer and winter with breaks in the shoulder seasons. She works with a lot of Kiwis chasing summer between the two countries, as she once did, and it can feel like she never left.

Her advice for getting into the industry:

* **Start small**, with summer camps or youth and school outdoor education, to learn to lead groups.
* **Build technical skills** on your own time, like whitewater and ski patrolling.
* **Have summer and winter skills**, so you can stay in one place for longer.

## Painting and illustrating the outdoors {#painting-outdoors}

On her second stint in New Zealand, Rachel did a lot of solo tramping and started painting. Watercolours were portable, and painting filled her solo evenings in the backcountry.

Her first painting she really loved came from near Blue Pools. She hiked in, saw how horrendously busy it was, kept walking, and painted a little gorge with a waterfall instead. For Rachel:

> "I love just the way that it made me observe more of the details of what I was seeing and what was surrounding me as a way to connect with where I was"

She was writing for a Canadian cycling magazine and Wilderness magazine, and when one article had no photos, she asked the Wilderness editor if she could send a painting instead. The painting took over from there, and the editor later suggested a monthly illustrated guide on outdoor skills, which is still going.

The deadline keeps her painting when she's tired, and topics are harder than the painting (cue 11pm messages to friends). With one page per guide, she revisits broad early guides for details worth their own page, and covers skills the rest of us assume everyone knows.

Next time she's back, Rachel wants to raft the Shotover, go on a multi-day [packrafting](https://abigailhannah.nz/196-packrafting-the-landsborough-river-from-the-west-coast-with-tom-waters/) trip, and race the Great Southern Brevet.
