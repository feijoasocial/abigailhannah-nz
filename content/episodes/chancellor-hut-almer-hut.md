I've had so many mums on the podcast talking about [adventures with their kids](https://abigailhannah.nz/126-adventures-with-kids-and-a-peach-cove-rescue-with-renata-wiles/), so I was really keen to hear from a dad. And Hami's take on it is so so clever.

Hami lives in Greymouth on the [West Coast](https://abigailhannah.nz/064-west-coast-road-trip/), and he gets out with his son and daughter most weekends. Tramping, beaches, lakes, rivers. For the last two summers, his family has treated themselves to a helicopter flight up to two of the most famous alpine huts in the country: Chancellor Hut above Fox Glacier and Almer Hut above Franz Josef Glacier.

So we get into both huts, the kea, the sunsets, a wee bit of snowboarding above a glacier and the logistics of booking a chopper. Then Hami shares his favourite family tramps on the Coast and the big goal on his bucket list. (I now have two more huts on my list. Thanks, Hami.)

## Hami and the West Coast {#hami-west-coast}

Hami's love of the outdoors goes back to school camps, and a week at an outdoor pursuits centre in the North Island in his last year of high school. In 2001, after high school, he moved to the West Coast to study outdoor leadership and guiding at the polytech for two years. He met great people, decided to stick around, and never really looked back.

He sums up living there like this:

> "I like to think we live in the world's biggest playground so we'd like to take advantage of that whenever we can."

## Chancellor Hut by helicopter with kids {#chancellor-hut}

Both huts had always been on Hami's bucket list. You can still walk to them, but it involves glacier travel, so it was never going to be possible as a family. He also felt a bit guilty about spending the money on a flight for himself. Then he realised a chopper fits four anyway, so why not take the whole family up and chill out together.

Chancellor Hut came first. It was the kids' first time in a [helicopter](https://abigailhannah.nz/001-helicopter-over-aoraki-with-billy-nunweek/): his son was pretty stoked, and his daughter not so much. They took earmuffs for the noise, and the flight was only five minutes.

Hami says Chancellor is New Zealand's oldest alpine hut still on its original foundation. It has 12 bunks, and patterned lining around the walls unlike anything he's seen in any other hut. Even in summer it's cold up there, so take plenty of jackets, pants and thermals.

They spent one night. With a helicopter you can take a few more luxury items (when they tramp with the kids, the cans of beer are usually the first thing to go), but you don't need much more than usual.

### Kea, Chancellor Dome and a sunset

The kea were a highlight, all over the roof. The other highlight for Hami was the walking track that carries on up from the hut, which sits on a tussock shelf. It leads up towards the névé and Chancellor Dome, a popular walk, with views of New Zealand's highest peaks and all the way down to the Tasman Sea. He spent an evening up there watching the sunset while the kids were supposed to be sleeping in the hut.

They had the hut to themselves that night. Hami wouldn't call Chancellor all that child friendly, though. It's close to the edge and the glacier, the terrain is uneven with lots of long tussock, and there isn't anywhere for kids to run around. If you're choosing between the two huts with young kids, he'd pick Almer.

The first time I shared a backcountry hut with a baby was at Sylvester Hut, and everyone there was super kind. [Backcountry huts](https://abigailhannah.nz/160-hut-etiquette-in-doc-huts-and-exploring-the-whitcombe-valley-with-renee-cadigan/) are a public service, and that space should be for everyone. Even in an adults-only hut you'll get snorers, so I take earplugs.

## Almer Hut above Franz Josef Glacier {#almer-hut}

I always get the two glaciers mixed up. Franz Josef is the one with the [Alex Knob](https://abigailhannah.nz/004-alex-knob-glacier-views-lucy/) walk, and Fox is further south.

One night at Chancellor didn't feel like enough, because they flew up in the afternoon and came down in the morning. So with a good weather window, they spent two nights at Almer. It's quite a bit higher and a lot further up the glacier, close to the névé, with views out to Centennial Hut and down to Castle Rocks Hut. From the hut you get amazing sunsets, you can see deep down into the crevasses, and you can hear ice falling at all hours of the day and night. Hami reckons the crevasses would be towering if you were down inside them.

They also took the kids snowboarding, an idea they got from Sonia at [Back to the Wild](https://abigailhannah.nz/015-old-ghost-road-family-backtothewild/). Most of the snow had gone, so they found a wee patch, and the kids learned to snowboard above the glacier.

On the flight up, Hami's partner held on tight to their daughter in the back, and Hami and his son sat up front. Almer is a bit safer for kids than Chancellor, but there are still jagged rocks everywhere and spots where they could slip and fall a few metres, so keep them close.

Food is usually Back Country Cuisine, and oats or Weet-Bix for his fussy-eater son. For Almer, though, they took up Domino's pizza.

## Booking a helicopter flight to an alpine hut {#heli-logistics}

Weather comes first. Before leaving Greymouth, Hami made sure it would be good both for the day they flew up and the day they wanted to come down. He checks multiple weather apps, like Yr, and the rain radars on MetVUW.

When we recorded, you could book on the day you wanted to fly. Living in Greymouth, they could drive straight down. The company told them to rock up, and if it wasn't clear they might have to wait around for an hour. If the weather hadn't been any good, they'd have stayed home and saved it for another weekend.

They agreed the return day and time when they booked. Both huts have a radio, so you can call down to the bases in the townships to change your flight time or get an earlier pick-up, weather permitting.

By Hami's maths, flying the whole family up to a hut and back costs less than a short scenic flight with a snow landing for four, and he thinks it's a way better option: it's cheaper, you spend more time in the mountains, and you can still do everything you want to in the townships.

### The glaciers are changing

For Hami, part of the reason to go now is how much the glaciers have changed since he first saw them as a kid. In his words:

> "I just often think what will these look like in thirty years time when my kids are my age … it's quite cool. You know to create these memories now for them."

Photos of Franz Josef from Alex Knob a few years apart show how much it's receded, and old photos from the 90s at [Tasman Glacier](https://abigailhannah.nz/029-tasman-glacier-guy-alisha/) are the same.

## Family tramps on the West Coast {#west-coast-family-tramps}

When they're driving south, Hami's family often breaks up the trip at Blowfly Hut, past Fox Glacier. It's a pretty flat track to a cool little hut, and he recommends it for families, though it's a big drive from Greymouth and in the middle of nowhere.

Closer to home, they're spoilt for choice. They've spent a couple of nights on the [Paparoa Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/168-from-first-tramp-to-paparoa-and-all-the-great-walks-with-nicola-harvie/), the newer Great Walk, and there's tons to do behind Hokitika. His rule of thumb: the track to the first hut on a track or up a river is usually pretty good and very doable for small legs. Past the first hut, it gets more overgrown and more technical.

His picks for a good intro to West Coast tramping:

* **Rapid Creek Hut** on the Hokitika
* **Dillons Homestead Hut** on the Taipo River
* **Kiwi Flat Hut** on the Waitaha

All of them have lots of river crossings, so be careful and make sure you've got a dry spell.

And about the rain. Hami's joke is that some parts of the Coast only rain twice a week: once for three days, then for four. But he's quick to add:

> "You will never see the West Coast on a sunny day on the news, only when it's raining, so we do get tons of sunshine days."

My first West Coast road trip was midwinter, on my first visit to the South Island, and I went days without seeing any sun. When I walked the [Old Ghost Road](https://abigailhannah.nz/solo-old-ghost-road/), I was prepared for rain the whole time, and most of it came overnight. And I once had a video go viral of Hokitika Gorge, bright blue in summer and brown in the rain with waterfalls pouring off the banks. Both are spectacular.

Hami agrees it's just as spectacular in a red rain warning. You can't just sit around and wait for the rain to stop.

## Hami's bucket list {#bucket-list}

Hami does a bit of trail running, and his big goal is to run up [Mount Aspiring](https://abigailhannah.nz/148-a-mountaineering-career-and-climbing-mt-aspiring-with-dom-huxley/). He saw someone else do it and naturally thought he'd give it a go too. He's certainly not doing it by himself, so he needs to find some friends who want to come.

With the family, he'd like to get up to Welcome Flat on the Copland for a couple of nights at the famous hot pools, which would be a good one to do in winter too.
