I'm so glad Bri finally came on the podcast for a chat. We met on the shores of Lake Pūkaki on a South Island trip, and I've loved sharing a few adventures with her along the way since. And you can't really talk about Bri without talking about Sunny, her Land Cruiser. That truck is part of her whole identity.

Bri is a photographer living in Wānaka. She grew up camping by lakes and beaches, spent a while in Mangawhai, then swapped the beaches for the mountains (no regrets yet). So we go right back to how Sunny came home, which involved a very long night of driving and a crash course in manual gears. Then her seven-week road trip from Cape Reinga to Bluff, getting bogged near Tākaka, her favourite 4WD trips around Otago, and what it really costs to keep a 70 series on the road. Plus how she makes the money side work, in her own words: broke but stoked.

## From camping holidays to Wānaka {#bri-story}

Bri did a lot of camping growing up. Summer holidays meant weeks at a time in a tent by a lake or the ocean, with no power and no phone reception. She loved it, and she reckons that's where her love of roughing it comes from.

Ask her beaches or mountains, and a year earlier she'd have said beaches. But since moving to Wānaka in July the year before we recorded, she's turning into more of a mountain girl. The ocean will always have her heart. The mountains give her constant joy: she wakes up and Roys Peak is right there, whereas at the beach, nine times out of ten there's a bitter wind.

Before that she spent about a year and a half in Mangawhai, and she misses it. But as cliché as it sounds, she feels she's found herself down south, with an awesome group of friends and a clear idea of what she wants to do with her life.

## Buying Sunny the Land Cruiser {#buying-sunny}

About two years before we recorded, just after the first lockdown, Bri went on a South Island road trip with friends in trucks with rooftop tents. That was the trip where we met, at Lake Pūkaki. She'd always wanted to do [van life](https://abigailhannah.nz/121-avoiding-vanlife-mistakes-and-life-on-the-road-in-nz-with-tiff-and-nathan/) and had been looking at vans to travel around New Zealand in. After that trip, and a couple more in the trucks later that year, she decided she could live the van life in a truck instead, and get to a few more places in a four-wheel drive.

A friend helped her find one, and she bought it sight unseen. There was one small catch: she'd never driven a manual.

### The long drive home

The day before the pick-up, her other car died. She and a mate flew down to Christchurch, picked the truck up on the Saturday morning and drove it north, her mate at the wheel, catching the ferry from Picton and reaching Wellington at about ten that night. At three in the morning they tried to sleep in a car park, until a security guard kicked them out at five. So they set off again in pouring rain, and decided this was the moment for Bri to learn manual, on quiet back-country roads.

She got it into fifth, went to change down, missed fourth and hit second. Everything seized up. The truck almost went into the ditch, then drifted sideways. It didn't stall, and it didn't roll, and Bri let her mate drive again. A week later she was driving manual perfectly fine.

I've had a few close calls on my motorbike, and I know how hard it is to get back on after something like that. It takes all your confidence away.

It took Bri a few days. Back in Auckland she practised at a sports park next to a fitness group, who watched her stall over and over, then cheered when she got going. Very stressful. But she had no choice: she lived 20 minutes out of town and needed to get to work.

### Why Sunny

Bri always loved the name Sunny, and thought she'd give it to a dog one day. Then the truck turned up, an off-white, yellowy, creamy colour, and a 70 series. Sunny the seventy. It fits.

## A Cape Reinga to Bluff road trip {#cape-reinga-to-bluff}

Back then, Bri worked at a cafe, did cleaning work on the side and picked up the odd photography job. Then came the big one: in March the year before we recorded, she set off on the longest time she'd ever spent away, after working six or seven days a week to pay for it.

The trip ran from Cape Reinga to Bluff. A friend joined her for the first week, and they went up through [Northland](https://abigailhannah.nz/176-a-locals-guide-to-northland-and-building-hiking-communities-with-hannah-whittaker/), spending their first night at Shipwreck Bay. Then they made their way to the very top, Bri's first time at Cape Reinga, and had a really fun [surf](https://abigailhannah.nz/070-learning-to-surf-in-new-zealand-with-amber-holyoake/) around the corner from Shipwreck Bay and Ahipara.

After about five days she swapped friends and headed south through Raglan, Wellington and Nelson to Christchurch, and on to [the Catlins](https://abigailhannah.nz/139-slow-travel-in-an-ev-and-catlins-dunedin-and-oamaru-with-mature-flaneur-tim-ward/) for the first time. From there she left everyone and drove down to Bluff alone, to get a photo at the sign and say she'd done Cape Reinga to Bluff. At the sign, at the other end of the country, she bumped into people from Mangawhai. New Zealand is too small.

When we were down that way, we skipped Bluff, because I'd read it wasn't the southernmost point. Then the southernmost point turned out to be a car park and a walk across what looked like paddocks, on a windy morning, so I skipped that too. I've now been to neither sign.

### Doubtful Sound, Mueller Hut and Milford Sound

After Bluff, four of them did an overnight cruise on Doubtful Sound, which was wicked. Then a rainy day in Wānaka, a place she'd only ever treated as a lunch stop.

At [Mount Cook](https://abigailhannah.nz/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/) she met up with a mate and a couple of his friends, and they walked up to [Mueller Hut](https://abigailhannah.nz/028-mueller-hut-backyard-travel-family/). Bri calls it incredible, and probably the coolest hike she's ever done. The next day the weather packed in, so they boosted off to Milford Sound. It was a big long drive, and worth it.

### Three extra weeks

The trip was only meant to last a month. Once she reached the South Island, Bri arranged more time, then found the ferries were fully booked for three weeks, so she got an extra three weeks on the road.

She spent some of it alone in the Catlins, where she made some really cool surfer friends and took some of her favourite photos ever of them surfing, then slowly made her way back north.

That balance matters. On her most recent road trip she had no time alone at all, and it was a struggle by the end. It's easy to get FOMO and say yes to everything, but her advice is to listen to yourself and recharge.

## Bogged near Tākaka {#bogged-takaka}

On the earlier trip, Bri and a friend left a campsite near Tākaka they didn't like the feel of, along a single-lane gravel road. A car came the other way, so Bri pulled over to let it pass, not realising the puddle at the side of the road was bog.

The truck went sideways. She jumped out and dug around in the mud to switch the hubs into four-wheel drive, and that still didn't get her out. So she ran down the road to get a farmer to tow her. He told her he'd done the exact same thing in the exact same place the year before, which made her feel a little bit better.

It does make you feel better. When we got stuck in Northland in [our van](https://abigailhannah.nz/137-a-5-year-journey-from-budget-diy-van-to-dream-home-on-wheels-with-abigail-and-isaac/), the guy who pulled us out said he'd pulled three people out of that spot the summer before, in much more capable vehicles, and wanted to know how we'd got a van down there at all.

Bri's answer to that one: with a lot of determination.

## Favourite 4WD trips around Otago {#favourite-4wd-trips}

Bri spent three months of the year we recorded living in the truck.

* **Mavora Lakes.** The year started on 1 January with a series of events that ended with an awesome group of people and a lot of trucks at Mavora Lakes. It was meant to be two or three trucks, then everyone joined, and it was so much fun.
* **Around Tekapo.** The week after (without Sunny), the adventures around Tekapo are still some of her favourite memories.
* **Skippers Canyon.** A couple of nights with a mate in two trucks, in beautiful weather. They got away from reception and spent the days walking while her mate attempted to fish. They also met a man who lived down there, who told them stories about gold mining.
* **The Old Woman and** [**Old Man Range**](https://abigailhannah.nz/106-overlanding-in-the-old-man-range-and-4wd-trips-with-jono-hitchcox/)**.** At the start of her road trip, four trucks went up both ranges. It was late January, there was still snow up there, and the views were absolutely insane.

She could keep going forever. Most people don't realise how many [4WD tracks](https://abigailhannah.nz/087-overlanding-in-new-zealand-up-the-godley-and-havelock-valleys-with-jack-laurent/) there are in New Zealand, and once you have a truck, a lot of doors open. Bri feels very lucky to be one of the few who get to explore those places:

> "We literally look around these valleys and be like I wonder how many people just have no idea any of this exists out here … every mountain range has so much to discover."

## Working as a photographer on the road {#photographer-on-the-road}

How does Bri fit so much in? Her motto, which she says all the time: she's broke but stoked.

She works for herself as a full-time [photographer](https://abigailhannah.nz/landscape-photography-meghan-maloney/). As any creative knows, work isn't guaranteed: it's a lot of emails, pitching and waiting on other people. When we recorded she was in a quiet period, with shoots in Auckland coming up. She's taken the plunge, and she thinks you need to throw yourself in the deep end and figure things out.

For this year's road trip, she moved out of her flat, left her job and set off with no savings. The plan was three months, turning around if the money ran out. Then:

> "the moment I got on the road trip. It just opened up all these new opportunities and I was able to find all this work that hadn't necessarily been there."

Most of her clients are in the North Island, where she knows more people, so building that base again from Wānaka was always going to be a challenge. When she got back she had one awesome photo contract and some shifts at the brewery where she'd worked the year before. She knows she can always make money if she needs to, so she's not saying no to adventures. If she can make it work, she'll do it.

I've come to the same place. Money comes and goes, and experiences happen once. Investing in yourself, or learning something, you get to keep forever. When money comes, be grateful, and when it goes, that's all right too.

### Life on the road, and coming home

Bri doesn't think she could live on the road full time in New Zealand: the country feels too small, a rooftop tent would be really hard year-round, and she's a bit of a sucker for the cold. Summers, though, she could definitely do.

Coming home after a long trip is a weird feeling, because on the road the truck becomes her home. After her last trip she moved into a new flat in Wānaka, carried her bedding in from the truck, set up her bed, sat down and thought: what now? It's nice to have a proper roof over your head, she says, and it's also nice being out in nature.

## The reality of owning an old Land Cruiser {#old-land-cruiser}

Sunny is Bri's daily driver and her only transport. When it's at the mechanic she rides her bike, and when she rode it to pick the truck up recently, the handlebars had somehow twisted round so the brakes sat above her hand. She couldn't brake properly and almost went over the top. A new bike is a later problem, for when she has money.

Her most recent road trip had the most problems she's ever had in one go:

* **The exhaust fell off** on the first day, so Sunny sounded very loud.
* **A broken leaf spring.** After a first week mostly on off-road tracks, a leaf spring broke completely on the way down to Macetown. The back end sagged and the suspension hit the chassis on every bump. The new parts had to be shipped north to the only place with the right tools, so for three weeks Sunny sat super low and Bri rode in her mates' trucks.
* **Death wobble.** After the suspension was fixed, braking made the truck vibrate viciously, which was kind of terrifying. A friend helped her replace the front brake pads and rotors.
* **A dead battery.** Her main battery went and she couldn't start the truck. It was under warranty, but she couldn't get it changed until she got back up to Mangawhai.

It was deflating. Every time she fixed something, something else went wrong, and she hadn't budgeted for any of it. But that's part of owning an old truck: every part will need replacing at some point, and it was unlucky that it all happened on the road. It all worked out in the end. On social media it looks like living the dream, and the reality can be pretty rough. Bri wouldn't change a thing, though: for her the pros always outweigh the cons.

## Dream trip: the South Island off-road {#dream-trip}

Bri's next big plan is another South Island road trip, because there's so much more to do there for four-wheel driving. Her dream is to get from the top of the South Island to the bottom almost entirely off-road, on station land, back-country roads and [4WD](https://abigailhannah.nz/128-4wd-adventures-across-the-length-of-the-south-island-with-rich-soult/) tracks, because a lot of it is connected. She needs to look into how much of it is possible, and there'd have to be stops in towns for food. But for the most part, it would be all mountain ranges.

Her parting words:

> "for anyone wanting to get into it prepare to have to spend money. But if you can get past that you can have some of the best adventures of your life."

The places these four-wheel drives can take you will blow your mind, she says. And on top of the places, she loves the community that four-wheel driving has brought together, with plenty of photographers in it too.
