Arthur's Pass is a region I overlook so so much on my travels, and Amelia is out there a couple of times a month, if not more. So I've been looking forward to picking her brain for a really long time.

Amelia (or Meals) is based in Christchurch and grew up in North [Canterbury](https://abigailhannah.nz/141-best-christchurch-and-canterbury-adventures-with-kids-with-jasmine-trafford/). She works full time in the heavy diesel trucking industry and escapes to the mountains on weekends, mostly Arthur's Pass. We first met almost exactly a year before we recorded, on a girls' trip organised through Instagram, during my big winter road trip with Isaac. I've been saying "you need to come on the podcast" ever since.

So this one is a proper guide. Amelia's favourite Arthur's Pass day hikes for beginners and beyond, the three attempts it took her to stand on top of Mount Philistine, what she'd tell anyone keen to try it, and her must-stops if you're driving through. And there's a lot about being the slow one at the back, which I relate to soooo hard (I'm a views-to-effort kind of girl too).

## How Amelia found the mountains {#amelia-story}

Amelia's childhood was really outdoorsy: skiing, water sports, camping, fishing and biscuiting. Then she grew out of it as a teenager, and it was all downhill until her 30s.

What brought her back was one photo on Instagram, of Peak Hill. It looks over Lake Coleridge in Canterbury, and the water was such an insane blue that she thought it couldn't be real. She needed to see it for herself. At the time, a hike that long sounded insane (who does that for fun?). These days it's nothing, and the lake really does look like that in real life. It took a while to get hooked, because Peak Hill is really type two fun. She eased in with some North Canterbury mountains, Mount Grey, Mount Richardson and Mount Thomas, which are reasonably easy hikes. Then she discovered Arthur's Pass, and that was it: these were the views she wanted.

### Getting out after a working week

Amelia finds the weekend missions so hard, mentally. Packing your stuff and getting in the car is the hardest part, and the driving gets harder as she gets older, especially with a big hike and the drive home on the same day. Unless she's the passenger princess. Then she could drive all day.

I have so much admiration for people who work Monday to Friday and get away every weekend, because when I worked nine to five, I needed to sit at home and do nothing. And even with all the flexibility I have now, our closest mountains feel so far to drive.

## Solo hiking and hiking with friends {#solo-or-friends}

At the beginning, Amelia would drag her boyfriend along, but he secretly hated it for ages and was too polite to say. So she started going by herself and found it wasn't so bad. You can go at your own pace, take all the breaks and eat all the snacks. She's pretty slow anyway, and if you don't make it to the top, you turn around and drive home. It's more chill.

Then she met people through social media she really enjoys hiking with, and that has been the best thing ever. Hiking with friends gives you someone to share the struggle with, or the mental load, or random chat to fill in the time. To her, the two are very different experiences.

I know the expectations thing well. Recently, on a sunrise hike, Isaac and I had packed the night before and slept in the van five minutes from the trailhead. Twenty minutes up the trail, I did not want to do it. He was really nice about it, but it's so much harder to turn around when it's not only you.

And when I walked the [Old Ghost Road](https://abigailhannah.nz/solo-old-ghost-road/), five days solo, I struggled with having nobody to reminisce with. It's just me, and a whole bunch of photos of moss.

## Where Arthur's Pass is {#where-arthurs-pass}

Arthur's Pass sits bang smack in the middle of the Southern Alps, two hours west of Christchurch, on the way to Greymouth. What makes it so great, for Amelia, is that you're driving along State Highway 73 and you pull over at the side of the road, and there's a mountain track right there. There's no big long valley approach. You start climbing straight away, and you have so many to choose from.

## Arthur's Pass day hikes for beginners and beyond {#arthurs-pass-day-hikes}

Amelia is definitely a day hike kind of gal. She likes to come back to a shower and her own bed. Here are her picks:

* **Otira Valley Track.** Her top easy one: you have to do this one at least. The car park is back towards Arthur's Pass from the Otira Viaduct lookout.
* **Devil's Punchbowl.** A half-hour walk, so if you have half an hour to spare, do it, and go all the way to the top.
* **Bealey Valley Track.** Really, really good, and great for bird spotting. Amelia has seen riflemen and tomtits there, which you don't often see in Christchurch, so it's always a treat.
* **Temple Basin.** Very steep up to the Lockwood Shelter, but once you're there, you can head off in all sorts of directions. It's a bit of an introduction to spicier ridgelines, up to Mount Blimit or over to Mount Cassidy. The views go for miles, and you get them from the start, with no hours of forest first.

Devil's Punchbowl is the one I have done, so I have homework. And a month before we recorded, I saw my first takahē in the wild on Tiritiri Matangi Island, after years of looking, so Arthur's Pass is my next spot for some birding.

### Overnights and riverbeds

Amelia has camped up at Temple Basin once in her tent, and she's walked to Crow Hut, up the Waimakariri Valley and back. That one involves riverbed walking, and she hates riverbed walking so much she never wants to do it again.

## Mount Philistine: three attempts {#mount-philistine-attempts}

Mount Philistine is meant to be a six to eight hour hike, Amelia says, which is why needing three attempts gets to her. It's unlike any other hike she's done, with tarns and bluffs, and it feels like another planet:

> "from the moment I've been on that mountain or tried to attempt it, I've just, it's consumed my brain. I just constantly think about it."

### Attempt one: a group of strangers

Her first try was with a group of people from social media she'd never met. They turned up at the car park and off they went. It was January and super hot, and Amelia doesn't do well in the heat. They'd started a bit later than she'd normally start a big day, and she forgot how much slower she is than other people. Not far in, she told them to go ahead, sat down with some food and water, felt better and caught them up on their break.

Next came the bluffs. She'd been up to that point before and looked at them thinking, how do you get up there? There's no visible track and they look very scary. But they were fine, and they ended up being her favourite part:

> "they were just a little bit spicy. But I felt safe and you could just see so far under you. And it was like exciting."

The group split. The faster ones went on to the summit, and the rest sat by a big, beautiful tarn and admitted defeat. They'd started at 9.30am, it was 3.30pm, and they were still so far from the summit. They waited for the others and descended into the evening. It was still a great day, but the mountain was bigger than she'd thought.

### Attempt two: a supermarket plan

Some time later, Amelia decided on a whim to take the next day off work and go for a mission. At the supermarket buying snacks, she ran into a friend from the first attempt, who got FOMO, applied for leave and hoped it came through by morning. Another friend from social media joined them. Amelia gave herself a head start, but they caught her so quickly.

This time they sidled around under the bluffs and up a gully, which is longer and meant to be easier. She found it a lot harder. She prefers short and steep, to get it over with. It was really hot again, and her body wasn't having it. She struggled more than the first time.

At about the same point as before, they worked out they still had three or four hours ahead of them to summit and get back to that point, so they made the call to turn back and swam in the tarn again. Amelia was frustrated. The people she was with were so capable that she felt she was holding them up, and they could have smashed out the summit (she'd have happily waited).

I really admire that she kept going back. Plenty of people will relate to this. When I did the [Milford Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/034-milford-track-nz-popular-walk/), my first time in a group of girls, I was constantly at the back, to the point they put me at the front to set the pace. And with daylight hours to think about, turning around both times was the right, safe call.

### Attempt three: third time lucky

Not long after, a different friend she'd talked to about Mount Philistine a lot messaged: let's go to Arthur's Pass this afternoon and climb it tomorrow. Could this be third time lucky?

After two failed attempts, Amelia knew what to change. They stayed in Arthur's Pass the night before, which saved two hours of driving, and got up at 4.30am. They started in the pitch black, it was a lot cooler, with a cold wind, and the pace felt better.

The only catch was the clag. Both earlier attempts had been bluebird days with views for miles. This time the summit was in and out of cloud all day. They kept climbing, unable to see where the summit was, and nine hours later, all of a sudden, she was standing on it. She was in disbelief, and if it hadn't been so clagged in, she probably would have cried. She didn't see a thing, so she's planning a fourth time.

### The long way down

The descent was slow. Her friend's knees didn't do well on the way down, and he got slower and slower as it got darker. Amelia had never hiked in the dark before, and it's hard. They had head torches and she had a backup torch, but she took them down the wrong spur, even after being up there quite a few times, and had to get them back to the right point.

Her friend had run out of water and was too nice to say so, or to ask for painkillers. She had spare water and painkillers the whole time. They got back to the car park at almost 9.30pm. Normally her feet would be so sore after a day like that, and this time she felt really good.

## Climbing Mount Philistine: route and prep {#philistine-route-prep}

Mount Philistine is 1,967 metres (Amelia checked her Topo app on air, and it opened on Mount Philistine).

The route starts on the Otira Valley Track, so you get a bit of a warm-up walking up the valley. Then you head off up some awful scree, then the bluffs, then a whole lot of alpine goodness. Only the Otira Valley Track is marked. After that there's a bit of a worn path, but there's route finding, and quite a few different ways you could go. Amelia doesn't think she could have done it by herself, and she wouldn't recommend going solo. You need a bit of experience.

When she climbed it, there was no snow. She's not super confident in snow, and with how scrambly and loose the rock was near the top, she prefers to see what she's holding on to. She'd love to go back in snow, but she'd definitely want crampons and an ice axe, and she's not sure how you'd fit it into a winter day with so little daylight.

Her prep tips:

* **Research online.** She did a lot of Googling and Instagram, and there are some good blogs about it.
* **Do the other Arthur's Pass mountains first.** Mount Bealey, Mount Aitken, Temple Basin, and Avalanche Peak (you have to do Avalanche Peak, it's kind of iconic).

## Slow hiking, DOC times and snacks {#slow-hiking}

Amelia has never been quicker than a DOC sign. She's usually doubling them, and reckons they must have an ultra runner making up the times.

I get irritated by that too, like when a sign says two hours and I'm there four hours later. The first time I matched a DOC time, I was so proud.

She does love steep. Distance is what she struggles with. She wants to gain elevation quickly and get up to the views.

Pushing through is really hard for her when her body hurts. So she takes constant breaks, sits, looks around and waits until she feels better, and sometimes the next break is five minutes away. Sugar helps: chocolate bars.

Seeing people on social media make hiking look so easy is frustrating, and she wonders why she struggles so much more. Even on the [Routeburn](https://abigailhannah.nz/009-solo-travel-routeburn-track-emily/), when we walked it together the winter before we recorded, she was at the back, and she's used to that.

That Routeburn trip was the first time I felt I was hiking at a normal pace. Usually Isaac and my girlfriends are streaming ahead. We had that beautiful snowfall at Routeburn Falls, then snow in [Mount Cook Village](https://abigailhannah.nz/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/) a couple of days later, which is going to be hard to beat.

## Canterbury stops and day hikes {#canterbury-stops}

If you're driving from Christchurch to the [West Coast](https://abigailhannah.nz/064-west-coast-road-trip/) and can't stop to hike, these are Amelia's must-stops:

* **Lake Lyndon**, and **Lake Pearson** if it's a nice day.
* **The Castle Hill Village toilets**, for the great view.
* **Castle Hill**, where all the cool rocks are, but go early, because whenever she drives past, the car park is full.
* **Devil's Punchbowl**, all the way to the top.
* **The cafe with the kea outside.** They do a good hot chocolate, and the kea are always hanging out at the toilets.

For the best day hikes in Canterbury beyond Arthur's Pass, she loves the [Mount Somers](https://abigailhannah.nz/hiking-mount-somers/) loop and Woolshed Creek Hut. She goes up Rhyolite Ridge, the way most people come down, because it's steep and you get the views quicker. She finds the other way boring.

Next on her list is the traverse between Avalanche Peak and Mount Bealey, which she's done separately.

I've had an episode about that [Avalanche-Bealey Traverse](https://abigailhannah.nz/104-avalanche-bealey-traverse-and-pursuing-adventures-with-neurological-disorders-with-nick-allen/), and Amelia had listened to it.

Her advice for anyone starting out:

> "This is so cliche, but you literally just have to go and do it. You just have to start small, kind of work out where your comfort zone is, what you do and don't like kind of thing."

The hardest bit is the mental side. Leave the house, drive towards the track, and if you don't complete it, it doesn't matter. You still had fun.
