Morgan is a listener. She heard me asking for guests, sent me a DM and said she wanted to come on and tell her story, and these are my favourite kinds of guests.

Her story is the best kind of chaotic. Back in 2019, after watching a YouTuber travel America out of her wagon, Morgan booked a ferry to the South Island that same night. She'd never driven alone somewhere new, never car camped alone, never even been to the South Island. A couple of months later she was off, with her dog Diego, in her station wagon.

So we go through the whole trip, raw rice and all. The mishaps, the highs, how she set up her car, freedom camping without a self-contained vehicle, and her tips for driving and car camping alone. Plus how that trip ended up moving her to Wānaka, and her advice for tramping with your dog. I love her attitude to things going wrong (I'm going to steal some of it).

## Booking a solo road trip on a whim {#booking-the-trip}

Morgan grew up in Upper Hutt, outside Wellington, and didn't do much travel as a kid. She'd never left the country.

She loved watching YouTube videos of [vanlife](https://abigailhannah.nz/121-avoiding-vanlife-mistakes-and-life-on-the-road-in-nz-with-tiff-and-nathan/) and people travelling everywhere, but always assumed you needed a van, which held her back. Then she found a YouTuber who travelled all the states in America out of her wagon. Morgan had a very similar car. So that night she watched all the videos and booked the ferry from Wellington to Picton, for a couple of months later.

Then she freaked out for two months, trying to work out how she'd actually make it work. She researched freedom camping spots, walks that allowed dogs and how to keep food cold. And then it didn't really go to plan, so she reckons maybe she should stick to being spontaneous.

I'm a bit of a planner, but I like to leave lots of space for spontaneous things. On our first South Island road trip, I knew where we were going and in which direction, but not how long it would all take.

She chose May (the whole month) so Diego wouldn't be in a hot car in summer, and to avoid winter. She didn't realise the South Island in May is a lot colder than Wellington in May.

Her work made the time off possible. She was working remotely for Shopify, and met colleagues in Canada who travelled around in vans, including a woman who worked from local parks on her motorbike trips. So Morgan saved her leave for the year and told work she was taking four weeks. She'd already booked the boat.

## A rough start: raw rice and a median barrier {#rough-start}

On day one, driving from Picton towards Nelson, she found a freedom camping spot in the middle of nowhere. She hadn't used her cooker before, and she's not sure why she left that to the first night of the trip. It was cold, the cooker didn't work, and she ended up crying in her car, eating raw rice for her first meal of the trip.

On day two, leaving a hike's car park onto the highway, she reversed fast to get going, didn't see a median barrier and got her car stuck on it. In the middle of nowhere, with no reception, with the sun going down.

She walked until someone took her to the nearest town, where a man was shutting up his petrol station and garage. He came back with her, hitched up the car and towed it off. He warned there might be damage, but the underside was perfect, her one bit of good luck that day. She thanked him with a box of beers, the true Kiwi way.

After that, there weren't too many muck-ups. There was a 5am lockout in the rain at one camping spot, waiting for the AA. But in the low points, she'd think about all the good things that had happened.

### The highs

Morgan ran an Instagram account for Diego at the time, and the friends she stayed with in Cromwell were people she only knew from dog Instagram. Now she counts them among her best friends. At a freedom camping site, another camper suggested a cool hike, so they went off and did it together. And her Instagram community rallied behind her through every up and down, which made her want to keep going and make the trip even better.

## The route around the South Island {#the-route}

She went through the top of the South Island and down the [West Coast](https://abigailhannah.nz/064-west-coast-road-trip/). The plan was to carry on down the coast into Central Otago and back up the East Coast. But storms had washed out bridges around Franz Josef, so there was no way down, and she cut across instead. She loved the drive through [Arthur's Pass](https://abigailhannah.nz/153-arthurs-pass-and-three-attempts-at-mount-philistine-with-amelia-hayes/).

I've done Arthur's Pass and Haast Pass, but not Lewis Pass, and I'm told I'm missing out. Morgan hasn't done it either, so it's on both our lists.

After Arthur's Pass she camped outside Christchurch for a bit, then headed down to Cromwell to stay with her friends for about a week, hanging around Cromwell, Queenstown and Wānaka. Then she headed back up the East Coast, through Christchurch, [Kaikōura](https://abigailhannah.nz/054-kaikoura-to-aoraki/) and Picton, and home.

## Car camping in a station wagon {#station-wagon-setup}

Diego is a Staffy cross dachshund, so he looks like a miniature Staffy. He's under knee height, the perfect adventure size, and he could hike all day. This was his first time car camping too, and Morgan wasn't sure he'd settle at night. He fell asleep in the bed straight away and was good for the rest of the trip.

Her setup:

* **Homemade curtains.** Cheap curtains from a second-hand shop, with velcro on them and on the roof of the car. The velcro came off the first night, so every night she was trying to stick them back up.
* **A small foam mattress**, narrower than her body.
* **Buckets of clothes and a chilly bin** on the other side, with everything else.

She had to slip in sideways, with Diego at her feet, so she always slept bent up. On the West Coast, a man came up and asked if she was really sleeping in there. He'd thought it was a bed for the dog while she drove.

She's since bought a bigger car, so next time she can at least have a single-bed-sized sliver of mattress.

On our first South Island road trip, also in winter, there were two of us in a van with a bed about the width of a king single. Some friends poked their heads in and couldn't believe we both fitted. It keeps you warm! Needless to say, our next vans had queen beds.

### What she was glad she packed

* **Thermals.** She'd never owned any before, because she never needed them in Upper Hutt. They were a lifesaver.
* **A psychedelic tie-dye coat for Diego**, because he was freezing all the time. Everyone loved it, so people kept coming up to talk to them, and she got to share what they were doing.

And the cooker? She used it the whole trip, but it took forever. Camping at Mavora Lakes with her Cromwell friends, who had the same cooker, theirs had dinner ready in minutes while she was still going. She learned to skip rice and stick to pasta.

## Freedom camping without self-containment {#freedom-camping}

Her car wasn't self-contained, which limits you a lot compared with a van. She used the CamperMate app, which lets you filter for places you can camp without a self-containment sticker, and planned roughly where she'd go. It's definitely doable. You just have to plan more and know where you'll end up.

[DOC campsites](https://abigailhannah.nz/camping-community-nz/) felt like luxury. She stayed at one privately owned paid campsite, and felt less safe there than at freedom camping spots: she kept the doors locked and her curtains up, worried someone would realise she was camping alone. She thinks it's because at freedom camping spots, everyone is doing the same thing, exploring as cheaply as they can. So after that one, she stuck to DOC and freedom camping. She wasn't going to pay to feel uneasy.

## Moving to Wānaka in lockdown {#moving-to-wanaka}

Looking back, that trip was a turning point. As Morgan puts it:

> "now I feel like looking back now that was the start of the change of my life."

At the start of 2020, she'd spent six weeks [house-sitting](https://abigailhannah.nz/151-house-sitting-across-new-zealand-with-travel-writer-jessica-lockhart/) a friend's place in Wānaka. She wanted to move, but thought people who lived there were just lucky, and she was scared of the cold. Maybe next summer, she thought.

Then came the 2020 lockdown, and being locked in made up her mind. She booked a ferry for the first day of Alert Level 3 and drove to Wānaka in one long day, with the car packed to the brim and Diego in a crate (he had the most room of anyone).

Her timing was great. Wānaka has a housing crisis, but in lockdown there were plenty of houses and no one to fill them. She got a six-month lease, with Diego, on the fanciest house she's ever been in, a triple-glazed new build. She books things and thinks about them later, she says.

She hasn't looked back. There are so many hikes and camping spots, and everyone's into the same outdoorsy scene. She hikes, camps and drives alone all the time now, a long way from her nine-to-five corporate life in Wellington.

I love her idea of house-sitting as a way to try living somewhere. Maybe I need to do a few house-sitting gigs around the country and see where I want to live next.

When Shopify made its first big round of layoffs, Morgan was made redundant. She loved that job, but it pushed her towards self-employment. When we recorded, she was in her first ski bum season, working at Cardrona for a tourism company, which everyone down there says is a rite of passage. After years of working from home, she loves working with people again.

## Tips for driving and car camping alone {#solo-road-trip-tips}

Morgan's first tip:

> "I think first off just do it just book it because you have to do it once you've booked it"

Once it's booked, you've got to follow through. Her other tips:

* **Do your research.** CamperMate was a great app to have. Get every app you can: ones that show petrol stations, walks and attractions are a lifesaver out there.
* **Download shows.** Get Netflix or YouTube videos downloaded, so when it's dark at 4.30 or the sandflies are attacking, you can hide in your car and watch something.
* **Take your time on the road.** On her first day, she was stressed by roads she'd never driven. Don't rush, and don't let the drivers behind you push you faster. If a train builds up, pull over somewhere safe and let them pass.
* **Focus on the positives.** Things will go wrong. Half her trips do, and the next several camping trips after this one went wrong too.

Even on that raw-rice night, she thought, at least I'm here. The next morning she woke up beside a beautiful river to the most gorgeous sunrise.

I love that the long winter nights were her excuse for a movie. On our winter roadie, the sun went down at five, dinner was done by seven and there was nothing to do but go to bed. The sun didn't come up until eight, so we slept twelve hours a night, and I've never felt so rested after a holiday.

On the driving, with the deaths and serious injuries on New Zealand's roads, driving to your own ability is so important. Our van does not go fast on the uphills, so we pull over often and let the trains pass, but only where it's safe.

## Hiking and camping with your dog {#hiking-with-dogs}

Diego is nine now, an old man, and he's taken so many hikes and camping trips in his stride. Morgan's dog tips:

* **Build up slowly.** Don't throw them into an eight-hour hike if they've never done one. If you'd need to train for it, so do they.
* **Get a light-up collar.** Her favourite thing she's ever bought for Diego. He's a small dark dog and hard to see at night or in the bush, so a rechargeable light-up collar goes on for early mornings, late evenings, long hikes and camping.
* **Use a long line if recall isn't 100%.** Diego has chased a goat or two, which taught Morgan his recall needed work.

One of their favourite spots is the Snow Farm, near Wānaka, which allows dogs in both seasons. In summer you can go through it into the Pisa Conservation Area and camp, among rolling hills and massive boulders that feel like Star Wars. In winter, if your dog has never seen snow, you can drive up and the snow is right there, and they rent out snowshoes.

I've had a couple of guests who've travelled with their [pets](https://abigailhannah.nz/020-travel-south-island-dogs-courtney/), so it was great to hear Morgan's take too.

Next up for Morgan is the winter at Cardrona with a season pass. She's a [snowboarder](https://abigailhannah.nz/014-snowsports-nz-olympian-anja-barugh/), who only learned when she moved down in 2020, and she reckons this is her year to get better. She'd also just got back from Hooker Hut, a lovely little hut with a fire so warm that no matter how cold it is outside, it's toasty. It's on my list.
