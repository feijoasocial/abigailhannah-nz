I needed a winter holiday. Something to look forward to, to carry me through the hard days at work and all the way to Christmas. So in the middle of a pile of work deadlines, I booked one, fast.

And this is a solo episode, so it's all me (I'm really excited about it). It's one week on the road out of Ōtautahi Christchurch: whales and seals in Kaikōura, a rainy detour through Hakatere Conservation Park, pies in Fairlie, Tekapo, and then Aoraki Mount Cook, one of my favourite places in the whole country. The forecast said rain all week. And we got so lucky with the weather.

I'll share how I fitted it around my 9 to 5, the te reo Māori words you'll hear me use, and the day-by-day itinerary. Plus the morning nine kea turned up at Sealy Tarns, which might be my favourite wildlife moment ever.

## Fitting a road trip around a 9 to 5 {#road-trip-and-work}

One thing I like to ask my guests is how they balance travel with work, so here's my answer this time. I work a 9 to 5 as an urban planner, and getting time off can be really hard.

For this trip, I used my company's flexible working policy. We flew down on a Friday and spent the weekend in Kaikōura. On Monday and Tuesday I [worked out of the Christchurch office](https://abigailhannah.nz/184-turning-new-zealand-work-trips-into-solo-great-walk-adventures-with-nick-thompson/), because my company has one down there. Then on Tuesday afternoon we hit the road, through to Sunday, and flew back up ready for work on Monday.

So three days of annual leave, spread over two weekends, got me a proper trip. I think I nailed it this time. I'm always looking for ways to stretch my leave around long weekends and public holidays, so if you've done something like this, send me all your tips.

## Te reo Māori words and place names {#te-reo-words}

We recorded during Te Wiki o te Reo Māori, Māori Language Week, so I wanted to share a few words you'll hear me use. I'm not Māori, and I'm by no means an expert. I'm on my own reo journey, and I think we all have a part to play in the revitalisation of te reo Māori. If you're fluent, or te reo is your mother tongue, please let me know if I get anything wrong, because I'm always learning.

* **Waka rererangi:** aeroplane
* **Haerenga:** journey or trip (a new word to me, and I love it)
* **Hīkoi:** walk
* **Maunga:** mountain
* **Whakarongo:** to listen (and you're all whakarongo to the podcast right now, so thank you for being here)

I also love using Māori place names. On this trip that means Aoraki Mount Cook, Ōtautahi Christchurch, Kaikōura (often said wrong), Hakatere Conservation Park, also called the Ashburton Lakes, and Te Waipounamu, the South Island.

## Kaikōura whale watching and seal colonies {#kaikoura}

### Whales from the air

On Saturday morning we picked up our self-contained campervan in Ōtautahi and drove north to Kaikōura, which is known for its wildlife. I'd never been whale watching, and you can go out on a boat, but a scenic flight felt like a more unique experience.

Kaikōura is incredible from the air. The mountains go almost straight into the ocean, and there was snow on Mount Fyffe, so it was beautiful before we'd even seen a whale. Not long after take-off, our pilot spotted a male sperm whale (there are only males in Kaikōura, which is super interesting). Sperm whales dive really deep, then come up to the surface for oxygen and hang around for a while, so the plane circled as long as it was up there. It was my first time ever seeing a whale, and they're so huge. I was so stoked.

### The seal colony and Ohau Point

On Sunday the clouds came in and hid the mountains and the snow. So we went on a hīkoi to the seal colony on the Kaikōura Peninsula, one of my favourite walks, because you can get so close. It's one of the largest mainland seal colonies in Aotearoa. You're in their habitat, so respect them and stay at least ten metres away. We could have watched them all day: the babies playing, and the adults snoozing and looking a bit sick of the kids.

Then we drove up the coast to Ohau Point, where there were more seals and pups jumping and diving into a little rock pool. There are so many ways to see the wildlife in Kaikōura. You can kayak with the seals and swim with dolphins too.

### Gore Bay on the way back

Heading back to Christchurch for work, we stopped at Gore Bay, where massive cliffs come down to the beach. It's worth a stop if you're going past, but probably not worth going too far out of your way.

## Hakatere Conservation Park and Mount Sunday {#hakatere-mount-sunday}

On Tuesday I knocked off work a little early and we beelined for Hakatere Conservation Park, taking the scenic inland route past Rakaia Gorge and its blue water.

The forecast for the rest of the week was really bad. I didn't want to go all the way to Aoraki and get rained on the whole time, so I even wondered about heading to Arthur's Pass instead, where waterfalls are more fun in the rain.

That first night we camped at Lake Camp, and it rained on and off all night. In the morning it was rainbows galore, sun showers everywhere.

Mount Sunday was the hike on the list, and it's in [Lord of the Rings](https://abigailhannah.nz/lotr-filming-locations/). I'm ashamed to say I'm not the biggest Lord of the Rings fan, and I get all the movies confused, so if you are a fan, you'll know which one it's from.

You know it's going to be good when it's at the end of a gravel road. You drive up between two valleys with completely flat valley floors, and there's this little Mount Sunday in the middle. We had to walk through a paddock of cows, which was a bit scary. The views were incredible, and the rain held off until we were heading back to the car, when we got a bit soaked.

We spent the afternoon around the park, saw a few more rainbows at Lake Heron, and it was way too windy for any more walks. With no signal to recheck the weather, we decided to keep moving towards Tekapo.

## Fairlie pies and Lake Tekapo {#fairlie-tekapo}

Of course we had to stop for a pie in Fairlie on the way through. The pies there are award winning, and they were definitely worth the hype.

Arriving at Tekapo, those Gatorade-blue lakes are exciting every single time. The only thing that comes close is watching people you love see Tekapo for the first time. We camped there that night.

In the morning we explored around Tekapo. I skipped the hike up Mount John because I've done it before. We went to the Church of the Good Shepherd, the stone church on the lakefront from all the classic Tekapo photos.

And I love the flying foxes at the playground there. If you want to feel like a child again, go on the flying foxes in Tekapo. Highly recommend.

## Lake Pukaki and Hooker Lake at sunset {#pukaki-hooker}

The next morning was absolutely beautiful, without a cloud in the sky, so we kept heading south to Lake Pukaki and Aoraki.

Oh my goodness, that feeling coming over the hill and seeing Lake Pukaki. There's a video of me absolutely losing it, I was so so excited. We had clear views of Aoraki, and it was all too much.

We took our time on the road into [Aoraki Mount Cook](https://abigailhannah.nz/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/), one of the most scenic roads in the whole country, and stopped way too many times for photos. Worth it.

We were camping at White Horse Hill that night. Once camp was set up, I suggested popping over the hill to see what was around the corner. A short pop over the hill turned into walking all the way up the Hooker Valley to Hooker Lake.

The sun was setting when we got there, and there was no one else around because it was so late. We were in this bowl of maunga, and Aoraki turned those pink and purple colours it does at sunset. Just to have good weather at all felt like a gift.

## Sealy Tarns at sunrise and nine kea {#sealy-tarns-kea}

### The stairway to heaven

We tried to get to sleep early, because the plan for Friday was Sealy Tarns for sunrise. The Sealy Tarns track is on the way to Mueller Hut, which I haven't done yet. When I had [Jen Parkes](https://abigailhannah.nz/028-mueller-hut-backyard-travel-family/) on, she shared how they hiked it as a family, with a three-year-old who managed it without too many tears.

Sealy Tarns is dubbed the stairway to heaven: 2,200 steps and 600 metres of height gain, straight up to the tarns. From there you get spectacular views of the Hooker Valley, Mueller Lake, Hooker Lake and all the peaks, including Aoraki and Mount Sefton.

I walk for the views at the end, and I usually regret it at that hour of the morning. But that three-year-old kept me going: if she could get up those stairs, I could too. It was still a pretty brutal hike with all that vertical gain.

On the way up, a kea screeched in and landed on the stairs behind us. Then he hopped up every step behind us, keeping a metre or two away. Buddy, you can fly! He had a tracking tag on his foot, and we fondly named him Alex.

The whole way up, the valley behind us got lighter as the sun rose, with magical colours across it. At the top, the views are phenomenal, across Hooker Valley and Mueller Lake and all the way out to Pukaki at the other end. My words can't do it justice.

### Nine kea at the picnic table

Then it got even better. A whole whānau of kea flew in around the picnic table up at Sealy Tarns. They kept moving so it was hard to count, but there were nine.

If you've never met a kea, they're alpine parrots, the world's only alpine parrot, and they hang out in the maunga of Aotearoa. They're super inquisitive and super cheeky. They played with the straps and buckles on our bags like toddlers, and tried to pull the CamelBak out. There's a "don't feed the kea" sign in the middle of the picnic table, and they were all having a go at ripping it off, as if they knew they stopped getting fed when it showed up.

Kea have this beautiful red under their wings when they fly, and we took so many photos and videos that we almost forgot about the view.

On the [Milford Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/034-milford-track-nz-popular-walk/) I saw four kea. This time, nine. Wildlife encounters in the wild are out of your control. My tip is to go at sunrise. On both trips I was up top at sunrise, and from my experience, people who came up later in the morning didn't get the same encounters.

## Tasman Lake and a slow last weekend {#tasman-lake-pukaki}

After breakfast at the bottom, we went round to [Tasman Lake](https://abigailhannah.nz/029-tasman-glacier-guy-alisha/) in the afternoon. There's a short walk to a viewpoint, and a longer walk down to the lake itself and the riverbed, where you can see icebergs floating. Pretty incredible.

Then we left Aoraki Mount Cook National Park and drove back round Lake Pukaki to freedom camp on the other side of the lake, for a chill-out Saturday. The weather was incredible again. In the middle of winter, I was in a skirt and a t-shirt, it was so warm.

And the reflections on Lake Pukaki were like nothing I've ever seen. I've been a couple of times, and I've never seen it as dead flat as it was that Friday and Saturday. Like glass. It's one of the most beautiful parts of the country, if not the world.

On Sunday we drove back to Ōtautahi, with another stop in Fairlie for another pie (and boy, was the line long on a Sunday), and the scenic inland route past Rakaia Gorge again. We dropped off the campervan, caught our waka rererangi back up, and I was back at work on Monday.

So yes, you can have a getaway in the middle of a busy, busy month of work. Use your leave around the weekends and time your flights right. And I wish you the best weather, and the best wildlife encounters, in Kaikōura and with the kea at Aoraki.
