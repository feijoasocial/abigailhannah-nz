Stacey and I met on a little community hike in Auckland, which was so fun. Then over summer I was somewhere snowy in America, watching her stories from New Zealand as she did tramp after tramp after tramp in the South Island. And I was like, that looks like such an epic trip.

Stacey works full time and gets out hiking in the weekends as much as she can. So when her work shut down over Christmas, she packed as many bucket-list tramps as she could into about 10 days: Earnslaw Burn, Routeburn Falls Hut and Lake Wilson, Mueller Hut (on what was meant to be a rest day!) and Brewster Hut, with a night camped next to the glacier.

So we go through each one, the long, long day out of Earnslaw Burn, how they got lost above Brewster Hut, her favourite piece of gear (and the mouse that ate it), and her advice for anyone with a long bucket list who doesn't know how to start ticking it off.

## How Stacey got into tramping {#stacey-story}

Stacey did a lot of day hikes as a kid, and her love of tramping probably started when her family walked the [Lake Waikaremoana](https://abigailhannah.nz/175-starting-tramping-in-her-40s-and-lake-waikaremoana-as-a-family-with-lois-baldwin/) track together, with everyone allowed to bring a friend. Then there was quite a big gap.

She really started hiking when she moved to Palmerston North for work. There's so much hiking around there, so she got out as much as she could and roped in a friend she lived with. They did the [Milford Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/034-milford-track-nz-popular-walk/), and now she goes as much as she can.

She's lucky to get most weekends off, which makes it easy to plan, and her reasoning is simple:

> "if I sit around and do nothing at home I'm just as tired as if I go out and do something so I might as well go and do something"

Around Palmy, she'd have a sleep in on the Saturday, do an overnighter, and try to get home early on Sunday to rest and reset for the week.

## Planning 10 days of South Island tramping {#planning-the-trip}

Stacey's company shuts down for two weeks every Christmas, and so does her friend's, because they work in the same industry. Stacey has a long bucket list, so she decided to fit in as much as she could, choosing hikes that were near each other and high on her list.

She's a planner (spreadsheets and all), so most of it was booked in advance. Stacey was initially going to go by herself, then her mum and her friend joined her. Her mum came for the first hike, Earnslaw Burn, then headed home, and the other two carried on together.

Plan A: fly down on Christmas Day, pick her friend up from the airport on Boxing Day and go straight to Glenorchy for Earnslaw Burn. Then a night in Glenorchy, the Routeburn with a side trip to Lake Wilson, a rest day in Cromwell, Brewster Hut, a day off and home.

## Earnslaw Burn {#earnslaw-burn}

[Earnslaw Burn](https://abigailhannah.nz/037-earnslaw-burn-gertrude-saddle-lauren-murray/) is just outside Glenorchy, and it's a long hike. A lot of people seem to do it in a day, and Stacey has no idea how. They took it slowly.

The forest is really pretty, but you're going up and down for hours, climbing over what feels like a million fallen trees. It keeps going on and on, and lots of people turned around because they realised it was too much.

There's no hut, so they camped. She found out afterwards that you're not meant to camp as far in as they did. There's a new sign with a camping spot just before it, and from there it's another hour or so into the burn itself. They'd camped a little further up the valley.

I know the rules for camping around Earnslaw Burn are a bit grey, with a mix of DOC land and private land. There are good blog posts out there, so check them before you go.

### The 11-hour walk out

The walk out took 11 hours, and that day was the low point of the whole trip. They kept going around corners thinking they must be almost back. They focused on getting to the meadow, knowing they'd be nearly there, but even that bit felt way longer than they remembered.

The hardest thing about Earnslaw Burn, for Stacey, is that there aren't many landmarks, so it's really hard to know where you're at, and that was mentally really draining. By the end she'd swapped her boots for her Tevas because her feet were so sore. She pushed on as fast as she could. They were very happy to see the car, and went straight to the hotel in Glenorchy for dinner and sleep.

My mantra for days like that is one foot in front of the other, over and over. Otherwise the feet don't want to move.

## Routeburn Falls Hut, Lake Wilson and Routeburn Flats {#routeburn-lake-wilson}

The next morning they had a bit of a sleep in, drove to Queenstown to drop her mum off, drove back and walked into Routeburn Falls Hut on the [Routeburn Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/009-solo-travel-routeburn-track-emily/). They were both pretty shattered and slow going, but they made it before six. The hut is incredible, with the waterfall right next to it and the view out the front. It gave them energy, and Stacey was running around taking photos the whole time.

### Lake Wilson

The next day they took a little side track off the Routeburn, through a really pretty little valley that's quite wet, so you've got to choose your spot. The climb up to Lake Wilson was really cool and a little bit sketchy: quite steep, with a bit of snow at the top, so you need good footing and to take your time.

The Routeburn was pretty busy, but up there only two other parties were camping near them.

### Routeburn Flats

They walked back out to camp at Routeburn Flats for the next night, because you can't camp at Routeburn Falls. The campsite is right next to the river, with massive mountains all around you and blue water running right through the valley.

From Routeburn Flats it's only about two hours to walk out. They drove to Queenstown for Fergburger, of course, then on to Cromwell, and spent New Year's Eve in bed recovering and doing their washing.

## Mueller Hut on a rest day {#mueller-hut}

The next morning, they were googling things to do in Cromwell. Not finding much, they went for a drive. Then they saw a sign for [Aoraki Mount Cook](https://abigailhannah.nz/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/).

A year or two before, the two of them had tried to do [Mueller Hut](https://abigailhannah.nz/028-mueller-hut-backyard-travel-family/) together and had to pull the pin. They'd always wanted to do it, but Stacey thought it was too far to add to their itinerary. On a whim, they checked for bunks, and there were two left. It's a three-hour drive, so they booked, went back, packed everything, drove down and hiked up. They started late, but in summer you have so many daylight hours.

As a planner, Stacey says her friend is good at getting her to commit, especially for a hut they'd wanted to do for so long.

When I did the Milford, at least three people in our group had booked the week before from cancellations.

Stacey's advice: if you're going to be in the area, check, because there are so many cancellations.

### The Mueller Hut track

You start at the White Horse Hill campground, in the same area as the start of the Hooker Valley Track, and walk for 30 minutes. Then it's the Sealy Tarns Track up to Sealy Tarns, and from there you're climbing over rocks. It's pretty steep. Some people were doing it up and down in a day, which she thinks is definitely doable in summer if you start early.

For once they could leave their tents in the car and stay in the hut, which they were so stoked about. They were pretty shattered by the time they got up. The legs were fine that day; it was going back down the next morning that they started complaining, and they stopped at Sealy Tarns for a sit.

I've been as far as Sealy Tarns, for sunrise on a winter day hike, and it's one of my favourite hikes.

## Brewster Hut and camping by the glacier {#brewster-hut}

They walked down from Mueller Hut, drove three hours and did Brewster Hut the same day. A massive day.

Brewster is probably one of Stacey's favourite hikes. It starts at the same car park as Fantail Falls on the Haast Pass. You cross the river and start climbing straight away, and it keeps going. Like Mueller, it's incredibly steep, but there aren't many steps. It's more climbing up tree roots, which she found easier than the steps, even though it was steeper. You're in forest for most of it, until you break out onto a ridgeline and carry on to the hut.

They couldn't get a hut booking, and later found out a big group had booked the whole hut, so there were never going to be cancellations. They'd planned to camp next to the hut, but the forecast meant they wouldn't get views the next morning, so they had a break at the hut and pushed on up to the glacier.

### Getting lost above Brewster Hut

There's no marked track between the hut and the glacier. A guy at the hut told them to follow the track up and head across at a certain spot. Their track ran out, so they headed across, climbed over rocks, and realised they weren't on the right way. Luckily a group of girls behind them, who'd followed the same path, found the route on Strava, got back on track and stood on a hill calling them up. They followed them to the glacier.

Stacey's tip: a lot of blog posts say there's no track and you make your own way, which confused them. There is a track, with cairns the whole way. If you're not on a track with cairns, you're going the wrong way. Once they were on it, they walked from one cairn to the next.

### A night next to the glacier

Stacey had never seen a glacier, and camping that close was mind-blowing. There was the big glacier with a little lake beside it, and they were camped slightly above. Every time she heard something in the night, she opened her tent to check they weren't about to be flooded out. It was still one of her two highlights of the trip. The other was finally seeing Earnslaw Burn with her mum, after talking about it for years.

The next day they walked down, drove to Queenstown to drop the rental car off by 5pm, spent the night in Frankton near the airport and flew home.

### How it felt

Stacey felt a bit of disbelief. Neither of them had really thought they'd be able to do it and feel okay. It wasn't until that last night, in a tiny hotel room with the beds right next to each other, that they realised they'd done it. And it's stuck with them:

> "we've noticed that we'll both push ourselves a bit more because we're like, oh, we actually know we can do it now."

## Tips for getting started and the Dusky Track {#tips-and-whats-next}

### Her favourite piece of gear

Stacey's new tent is less than a kilo. After an old, heavy one, she was so grateful to have it. Then a mouse ate a couple of holes in it.

### Getting started

Stacey's first piece of advice:

> "not to wait for other people because I think that was something that held me back at the start"

She started doing a lot of hikes alone, found friends through that, and last year probably did 80% of her hikes alone. Hiking on your own can be scary, though, so:

* **Start with day hikes**, especially ones in your area, and slowly build up.
* **Try the** [**Great Walks**](https://abigailhannah.nz/10-great-walks-in-10-days/) **for your first overnighters.** You can carry less, because the huts often have gas, and the wardens can help with the weather and check you've turned up. They're Great Walks for a reason.
* **You don't need the fanciest gear.** Everyone starts somewhere, and you can hire it.
* **Take a** [**PLB**](https://abigailhannah.nz/plb-safety/) if you can. You can hire them pretty cheaply, and it's peace of mind.
* **Reach out to hikers on Instagram** if you're nervous about going alone, especially as a woman. Stacey is happy to take people out any time, and lots of people are looking for new hiking friends.

My second overnight tramp was on the [Tongariro Northern Circuit](https://abigailhannah.nz/172-hiking-the-tongariro-alpine-crossing-and-tongariro-northern-circuit-with-ben-boland/), and I wholeheartedly agree on the Great Walks. And if you don't have gear, ask around. People will be happy to lend you theirs.

Stacey's tip for me: do the Routeburn next. It's one of her favourite hikes ever.

### Next: the Dusky Track

The [Dusky Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/049-dusky-track-fiordland/) is really high on Stacey's bucket list. It looks absolutely incredible, and it seems like one of those hikes that might be absolutely horrible while you're doing it: she's heard there's knee-deep mud and sandflies. She's been slowly putting it off, but after her Christmas trip she thinks it might be possible soon.

I've seen the tiny Dusky Track sign on the road into Doubtful Sound, pointing straight down the hill into the bush, and decided I'm not ready. Maybe never.
