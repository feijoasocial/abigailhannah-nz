So many visitors come to Aotearoa every year, and I really really wanted to hear what this place looks like through fresh eyes. Tracy had wanted to visit since she was a child. She finally made it in her mid-50s, and she still can't get New Zealand out of her head.

Tracy is Australian, she's travelled to numerous countries and lived all over the world, and she runs travel planning podcasts. She's also very clear that she is not a hiker. Which makes her day on the Hooker Valley Track my favourite part of this chat.

And I'd always thought my listeners would skew 25 to 35. But there are listeners all the way into their sixties, so I'm soooo glad to have Tracy's take on travelling here.

So here's her trip, start to finish. A few days in the North Island, with Hobbiton and the Rotorua geothermal parks. A house sit in Christchurch, the TranzAlpine, the Hooker Valley Track, Wānaka, Queenstown and Milford Sound. Plus her tips for house sitting your way around the country.

## Planning a first trip to New Zealand {#planning-first-trip}

This was Tracy's first time in New Zealand, after wanting to come since she was a child. By the time we talked she was back home in Australia and about to leave on a five month trip, and she was still thinking about this one:

> "I must admit, New Zealand is it. It just has really got under my skin"

The trip had to work around her husband's job. At first it looked like the two of them would fly out from Brisbane on a Sunday night and fly back on the Friday. Tracy can work from anywhere, though, and that wasn't long enough for her. So she started looking for house sits, and she was very lucky to find one in Christchurch. That meant she could see both islands.

The North Island part was short, so she had to work out her priorities: a day in Auckland, a trip to Hobbiton, and Rotorua.

## Rotorua geothermal parks and Hobbiton {#rotorua-geothermal}

From Auckland they drove to Matamata for [Hobbiton](https://abigailhannah.nz/lotr-filming-locations/) ("because who doesn't"), then spent two days in [Rotorua](https://abigailhannah.nz/038-drone-photography-rotorua-petra-leary/).

Rotorua was high on Tracy's list because of a childhood memory. If you grew up in the UK in the 70s and early 80s, you might remember an episode of Blue Peter that came from Rotorua. She remembers the geysers, and she'd never seen anything like it. She never forgot it.

Now Rotorua is in her top destinations. She fell in love with the [geothermal](https://abigailhannah.nz/north-island-hidden-gems/) features. Her stops:

* **Te Puia** was the first place they went, with its geyser and the Māori Arts and Crafts Institute. She absolutely loved it.
* **The kiwi hatchery** filled the morning of their busiest day.
* **Wai-O-Tapu Thermal Wonderland** came next. Tracy was deciding whether to walk the whole way round, because they had a busy day, and the woman sorting their tickets at the entrance told her to do the whole thing. She was right.
* **The Polynesian Spa** finished that day off. After days of intense sightseeing, she says, what a way to end.

Of Wai-O-Tapu, she says:

> "I could have walked around that and round that and round that all day."

Rotorua is one of my favourite places too. Every time I visit, the geothermal stuff makes me feel like I'm on the surface of Mars, with all the colours in the lakes. Wai-O-Tapu was the first park I went to, and I've been to Hell's Gate as well.

Tracy is planning to go back, and if she can get a house sit in Rotorua, she'll be there.

## A Christchurch house sit and the TranzAlpine {#christchurch-tranzalpine}

The South Island was the longer part of the trip, based at the house sit in [Christchurch](https://abigailhannah.nz/141-best-christchurch-and-canterbury-adventures-with-kids-with-jasmine-trafford/). Her daughter flew out and spent a week with her, and they went to Akaroa for the day, which was fantastic.

Near the end of the house sit, her husband flew back out, and they took the TranzAlpine across [Arthur's Pass](https://abigailhannah.nz/153-arthurs-pass-and-three-attempts-at-mount-philistine-with-amelia-hayes/) to Greymouth and back. They both love train travel, and the TranzAlpine was high on his bucket list. Tracy gives a huge shout-out to the staff on the day they went: they were all fantastic. With her husband tired from his flight, it made for a very relaxing day.

I haven't done the TranzAlpine yet. I've driven the road and seen it go past, and it looks like it would be an incredible journey.

The next day they picked up a hire car at the airport for nine days of driving through the South Island. They headed for Lake Tekapo and Lake Pukaki, and stopped at the Fairlie Bakehouse on the way, because everybody told them to.

I went to the Fairlie Bakehouse for the first time the month before we recorded, and they delivered. Great pies.

## Hooker Valley Track for a non-hiker {#hooker-valley}

The night in Aoraki Mount Cook Village was a last-minute change, after talking to the people Tracy was house sitting for, and she was very very lucky to get a cancellation. The week before, her daughter had taken a day trip there from Christchurch on a beautiful blue-sky day. Tracy saw the photos and decided they had to go.

The track was closed the day they arrived. The next day was blue sky, so they walked the [Hooker Valley Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/aoraki-mount-cook-national-park/).

Tracy is not a hiker. It's one of her mantras. Her daughter loves hiking, and on the drive they had a huge discussion about why she enjoys it, and why Tracy would too if she'd give it a go. So the Hooker Valley was a let's-see-how-we-get-on walk.

She was a bit concerned about the swing bridges, because she's not great with heights. There are three of them. When she made it to the glacier lake at the end, she was so proud that she took a photo and sent it off: look where I am standing. Her verdict:

> "I would do it again … in a heartbeat absolutely in a heartbeat"

Her tips:

* **Start early.** They were in the car park fairly early and had the track to themselves, with photos and videos of nobody else on it. On the way back, there were so many more people.
* **Expect questions.** By the end, people kept asking them how far there was to go. It's not well signposted, and Tracy would like the odd marker showing how far you've still got, for those who don't love a kilometre.

I think counting down the swing bridges would help. It's almost harder coming back, because you're sure it was quicker on the way there, and how far is the car park?

Tracy has spent time in Norway, which she adores, and lived in Switzerland. Even so, the Hooker Valley is definitely in her top four or five experiences of her entire life, along with the Rotorua geothermal features.

I wholeheartedly agree. The Hooker Valley Track is my top walk in Aotearoa. It's so accessible: I've seen kids on the track and people of all levels of fitness, and you can walk right up to look at Aoraki, the tallest mountain in the country. If Tracy, as a non-hiker, would do it again, then absolutely anyone can do it.

## Wānaka, Queenstown, Glenorchy and Milford Sound {#queenstown-milford}

They spent one night in a hotel in Aoraki Mount Cook Village and one in Wānaka, which Tracy would very much like to go back to. They took a picture of the tree in Wānaka, then on the way to Queenstown popped into a winery that had been recommended the night before. They planned on half an hour, then decided to stay and enjoy the cheeseboard, the food and an excellent wine tasting, and Tracy left with bottles. Her husband did all the driving, so she could enjoy the scenery.

[Queenstown](https://abigailhannah.nz/189-moving-to-aotearoa-from-chicago-and-building-a-life-in-queenstown-with-viktoria-de-dominicis/) was everything she expected, and she'd chosen it as a base for trips. The last couple of days were rainy and cloudy, and the season was finishing, but there are plenty of indoor things to do.

Their day trip to Glenorchy, with the red shed, got a beautiful day. It's another lovely drive, and they finished with a coffee at the coffee shop before heading back.

I agree, it's a beautiful drive to Glenorchy.

Milford Sound was an all-day coach trip on the Saturday, with a coffee stop in Te Anau each way. The weather wasn't good, so they had to get quite close up to see anything. Tracy is a lover of the Norwegian fjords, and because of all the time in the coach, she says next time she'll save up for a helicopter or plane flight. She's been told Doubtful Sound is fabulous too.

What made the day special was their coach driver, who is Māori. He kept everyone amused with stories about his childhood, his upbringing and living in New Zealand, and that's one of Tracy's special moments from the trip.

You can also catch a bus into Milford Sound from Te Anau, which would break up the day and make it a bit shorter.

## House sitting around New Zealand {#house-sitting}

Tracy and her husband work online, so house sitting gives them a base to catch up on work without doing it in a hotel. As long as there's good wifi, it's perfect. Their dream is to house sit their way around New Zealand, ideally twelve months through the North and South Islands. For Tracy, the joy is in staying longer, immersing herself, doing the ordinary shop, learning the area and being like a local, along with looking after other people's animals and taking dogs for walks.

Her advice for getting started with [house sitting](https://abigailhannah.nz/151-house-sitting-across-new-zealand-with-travel-writer-jessica-lockhart/):

* **Join a global network.** Tracy uses TrustedHousesitters, which has sits all over the world.
* **Start local.** They began with house sits in their own area to build up references.
* **Plan ahead.** When we recorded, she'd applied for a sit in the Cotswolds for December and January, as part of her five month trip through Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand, the UK, India and Singapore.

It reminds me of The Holiday, where they swap houses and one ends up in a mansion in the States and the other in a tiny cottage in the UK. There's beauty in the small things, and in a place as beautiful as the South Island, your local park around the corner is probably a beautiful spot too.
