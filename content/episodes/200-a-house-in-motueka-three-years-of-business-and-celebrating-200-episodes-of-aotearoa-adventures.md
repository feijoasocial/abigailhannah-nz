Two hundred episodes. I still can't quite believe I'm typing that.

So this one runs a little differently. It's just me, there's no adventure story (there are 199 other episodes for that), and I recorded it from Cape Town, where Isaac and I were for a friend's wedding. It's a celebration of what Aotearoa Adventures is, plus some really really big life updates.

The biggest one: after two and a half years with no home address, we've signed a lease on a house in Motueka. I'm soooo excited.

This post covers how the podcast started and what 200 stories have done for my own adventures, three years of running my own business, why we picked the top of the south, and what's next for the show, including the written articles you're reading right now. Plus the best day of my trip (whales, lots of whales) and some thoughts on social media.

## Celebrating 200 episodes of Aotearoa Adventures {#200-episodes}

I've hosted all 200 episodes, and we ticked over the [four-year milestone](https://abigailhannah.nz/183-four-years-of-aotearoa-adventures-and-no-home-address-with-host-abigail-hannah/) earlier this year. At 100 episodes, we threw a big party in Auckland with a live podcast event, where a listener turned guest, [Olly Dow](https://abigailhannah.nz/100-celebrating-100-episodes-of-the-aotearoa-adventures-podcast-with-abigail-hannah-and-special-host-olly-dow/), interviewed me and the audience asked questions too. Most of my recordings are virtual, so meeting guests in real life was so cool.

I started the podcast after a six-month road trip in the South Island, heading back into my corporate job and needing to live vicariously through other people's stories. It was an excuse for some good yarns. Here we are, 200 yarns later.

At about 50 episodes a year, episode 250 lands next year. Maybe a party for that one.

## How guests' stories changed my own adventures {#guest-stories}

What I didn't expect when I started is how much longer my bucket list would get, and how empowering these stories can be.

One of my early guests was [Emily](https://abigailhannah.nz/009-solo-travel-routeburn-track-emily/), who did a solo hike on the Routeburn. Since then I've talked to so many women who've done massive solo missions, on Te Araroa, the Great Walks and multi-day trips in the backcountry. Tanya comes to mind, who did the Fiordland Traverse. And [Red](https://abigailhannah.nz/nz-kayak-circumnavigation/), one of my heroes, who circumnavigated the entire country in a kayak. It took her forever, but she did it, and she mentored us on our own kayak journey.

Without Aotearoa Adventures, I wouldn't have done my first solo hike, on the [Old Ghost Road](https://abigailhannah.nz/solo-old-ghost-road/). I did it because of hearing other people's stories. (I got emotional about five minutes into recording this one.)

Same for finally [building out our van](https://abigailhannah.nz/137-a-5-year-journey-from-budget-diy-van-to-dream-home-on-wheels-with-abigail-and-isaac/) and living full time on the road, and for our two-month [sea kayak expedition](https://abigailhannah.nz/182-finishing-a-two-month-slow-paddle-sea-kayak-expedition-with-isaac-and-abigail/), with its many setbacks. So much of our life now came from other people's stories. If you've been one of the past 199 guests, thank you so so much.

That's what I love about podcasts as long-form content: an episode from three years ago tells the same story today. I'm so sick of seven-second reels telling you to visit a place with nothing about why.

## Three years of running my own business {#three-years-business}

Labour Weekend 2023 was my last day at my corporate job. I studied urban planning and worked as an urban planner, at an incredible company with such good culture.

I knew I didn't want to be in one place for the rest of my life, and urban planning kind of means that. Isaac is studying an outdoor education degree, which can take him anywhere in the world, kayak guiding, hiking guiding, scuba or ski instructing. I wanted a career I could take anywhere too. I'd been doing the Instagram thing for a while, so I [quit my corporate job](https://abigailhannah.nz/bonus-i-quit-my-corporate-job/) and took the leap.

When I recorded, Labour Weekend was seven days away: three years of a business that's mine. I was only at that company for about 18 months, so I've now run my business for twice as long.

### Becoming a company director

I was a sole trader for a couple of years, and earlier this year I incorporated Feijoa Social as a limited company. I was filling out forms at my computer one day when it hit me that I'm the first woman in my bloodline to be the sole director and shareholder of a limited company. I looked at the company register, saw my name, and had a full breakdown. Not that long ago, women couldn't own houses or land on their own, and further back they couldn't vote. I feel so honoured to be alive at a time when I can start a limited company.

What I do, if you're new here, is social media marketing for travel and outdoor brands. Dream, right? It's been a roller coaster: after the slow paddle, back in April, I had to rebuild my business.

Being a founder means being the accountant, the marketing person and the manager of a team all at once, and it can be somewhat isolating. I've also found it tricky to share my business journey without it coming across as bragging or insensitive. I'm deeply aware of the privilege I've had, and it's been hard work too. This year has gone so well that I'm a little scared something will go wrong.

## Moving to Motueka in the top of the south {#moving-to-motueka}

Here's the big update. Isaac and I have signed a lease on a house in Motueka, our first permanent address in two and a half years. We've slept in a lot of beds in that time. For this next season, we want to be in one place, and it's a pretty big deal for us.

### Why the top of the south

We have a lot of friends in Wānaka and Queenstown, and I always thought we'd live in the mountains down there. But there are no beaches. As kayakers coming from Auckland, where there's amazing beach everywhere (I'm going to miss Auckland), we wanted the coastline. Isaac has a summer job as a sea kayak guide in the [Abel Tasman](https://abigailhannah.nz/026-abel-tasman-kyle-mulinder/).

And we so so love the top of the south. I'm a little scared to tell everyone how amazing it is.

The summer after I quit, we spent [six weeks just in the top of the south](https://abigailhannah.nz/192-six-week-road-trip-in-the-top-of-the-south-island-with-abigail-isaac/). It was one of the best trips we've had, with so much to do that we didn't need to go further.

- **Old Ghost Road**: that was the summer I walked it solo.
- **Murchison**: Isaac went [white water kayaking](https://abigailhannah.nz/099-moving-to-new-zealand-and-learning-to-white-water-kayak-with-isaac-naylor/) there.
- **Karamea**: we went up the West Coast to Karamea. The [Heaphy Track](https://abigailhannah.nz/133-tramping-the-heaphy-track-from-karamea-to-collingwood-with-jacq-nesbit/) was my other option if I didn't do the Old Ghost Road.
- **Golden Bay**: we went [rock climbing](https://abigailhannah.nz/143-intro-to-rock-climbing-and-nzs-best-crags-with-josh-parker/) at Paines Ford, and that whole region is amazing.
- **Queen Charlotte Sound**: a couple of nights sea kayaking.
- **[Nelson Lakes](https://abigailhannah.nz/016-nelson-lakes-mel-white/)**: [Angelus Hut](https://abigailhannah.nz/039-angelus-hut-dylan-arthur/) and all those mountains.

I also walked the Abel Tasman that summer, which was super epic.

### Adventures on the doorstep

There's so much in that area: Pelorus, the [Richmond Ranges](https://abigailhannah.nz/193-solo-tramping-the-richmond-ranges-and-running-an-outdoor-store-with-emily-thompson/), Nelson Lakes, [Kahurangi](https://abigailhannah.nz/165-an-18-day-mission-through-nelson-lakes-kahurangi-and-abel-tasman-with-the-national-park-trifecta/) and the Abel Tasman, plus the [via ferrata on Tākaka Hill](https://abigailhannah.nz/199-building-nzs-first-public-via-ferrata-on-tkaka-hill-with-till-middelhauve/) from the last episode. We're so excited for long evenings, clocking off work and going for a hike or an overnight.

The lease is for 12 months. I'm also looking forward to setting up an office of my own where I can lock in, and to a second screen after working off a laptop for so long.

## What's next for the podcast and the website {#whats-next}

### Taking the podcast to video

I've been toying with moving the podcast to video, and I've pushed back for a long time. The stories are about incredible landscapes, oceans and mountains, and a video of me talking can't capture that. Also, I record in my pyjamas, from the van, or at 6am when you really don't want to see my morning hair.

The podcast industry is moving towards video, though, and being in one place means I could set up a bit of a studio space. So tell me if you enjoyed seeing my face on this one.

### Written articles for every episode

I've always wanted long-form blog posts alongside every episode. The main reason is accessibility: plenty of people can't listen to podcasts, or are hard of hearing, and I want Aotearoa Adventures to be accessible to everyone. And it's hard to find one detail from an episode you heard 50 episodes ago.

The podcast is still a passion project, and turning an hour-long conversation into a 2,000-word article is not a quick job. After talking to so many copywriters, it wasn't feasible.

So, please don't hate me: AI is finally at a stage where it can do this well. It rewrites the stories we already tell on the show, with no ideas of its own. I didn't want AI slop, and the articles so far tell the story how it is.

The articles are going up on abigailhannah.nz, and you can search the site for any topic or person, from Tongariro to skiing, and find the relevant episodes.

### Guests, questions and more of our own adventures

Our broadcast channel on Instagram lets you ask upcoming guests your questions. And if you or a mate has an epic story, slide into my DMs. [Surfing](https://abigailhannah.nz/learn-to-surf-new-zealand/) and [sailing](https://abigailhannah.nz/025-sailing-stephanie-haden/) have only had brief coverage, we've had a couple of [packrafting](https://abigailhannah.nz/196-packrafting-the-landsborough-river-from-the-west-coast-with-tom-waters/) episodes recently, and it's been a while since we had van stories (some people love them, some people hate them).

When I [paused the podcast](https://abigailhannah.nz/were-hitting-pause/) earlier this year and asked for feedback, some of you said you wanted more of my adventures and Isaac's. I'm so touched. As we explore the top of the south, we'll keep sharing our stories.

## Whale watching in Hermanus, South Africa {#hermanus-whales}

This one's from South Africa, so technically it's outside the rules for Aotearoa Adventures. I get to break them. It was one of the best days of my life.

Hermanus is one of the best places in the world to whale watch from the land. After two months on the slow paddle, where we saw whales once and no dolphins, my hopes weren't high (maybe they don't like me).

We day tripped there from Cape Town, and at lunch overlooking the sea I spotted something in the water. Out came the binoculars: a southern right whale.

At another spot that had been recommended to us, I saw a whale before we were even out of the car, and Isaac didn't believe me. There were two more southern rights, splashing about. Their flippers are quite square, compared with a humpback's long ones. With their heads out, they looked like orca, white patches and all. Then, behind them, a mama humpback did a massive breach, and her baby did a little breach next to her. We watched for a couple of hours: tails, flippers, spouts, breaches.

Back in Hermanus, from a third-floor bar, we watched humpbacks breaching in the bay, so close to shore. If you ever go to South Africa, go in October and go to Hermanus.

I wanted whales and penguins that day, and we got both: Betty's Bay, a little less touristy, has African penguins, babies included.

## Social media, the camera and getting outside {#social-media}

If you've followed me for a long time and noticed I haven't been as active on socials lately, this might be why.

When I started on social media, I picked up a camera, and the camera got me out to places, like Cathedral Cove for a photo or Piha for golden hour. If you know my story, you'll know I was diagnosed with depression, and it was a really rough part of my life. Getting outside was a big step in that healing journey, and the creativity of shooting and being outside did wonders for my mental health. That's how I started to see Auckland, how we had our first road trip to the South Island, and how we discovered the Coromandel, the East Cape and [Northland](https://abigailhannah.nz/176-a-locals-guide-to-northland-and-building-hiking-communities-with-hannah-whittaker/).

Now I leave the camera at home, and I'm not inspired to share. The algorithm feels so clickbaity, and you don't see the journeys of the people you follow.

### Detoxing my Instagram feed

I've detoxed my Instagram feed, and I barely get any ads now (please don't tell Meta). Every time I see an ad, I tap the three dots and say not interested, and I do the same for anything suggested from someone I don't follow. Ads feel predatory to me, because they know your insecurities. Now opening the app gives me a lot less anxiety.

Part of why I've stepped back is that I don't want to be another person telling you that one trick will let you quit your job tomorrow. I have quit my job and built the life of my dreams, though, and it feels selfish not to share that. A lot of content online is sensational and frankly false. I didn't get here in a day. It's taken three years.

So if you want to change your life for the better: it's possible, it's probably harder than people online say, and there's probably someone who's done it you can take inspiration from.

I'm passionate about educating creators too. I teach creators about pricing and partnerships in my course on brand partnerships, and two people from it have quit their jobs to become full-time photographers. Whatever your dreams are, they don't have to look like mine. In 199 stories you've heard from people with full-time jobs (an unreasonable number of doctors) who still find time for epic expeditions. Whether you want to live in a van, have more adventures with your kids or travel the world with a laptop, it's possible.

My DMs and emails are always open, and your messages make my day. If I leave you on seen, I'm probably crying and processing it for a week first. Thank you for supporting Aotearoa Adventures for four whole years.
